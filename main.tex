\title[Partial Least Squares for Correlation Matrices]{Partial Least Squares for Correlation Matrices: Theory and Applications}

\author*[1,2]{\fnm{Paul} \sur{Saves}}\email{paul.saves@ird.fr}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{UMMISCO}, \orgname{IRD, Sorbonne Universit\'e}, \orgaddress{\city{Hanoi}, \country{Vietnam}}}
\affil[2]{\orgdiv{IRIT}, \orgname{Universit\'e Toulouse Capitole}, \orgaddress{\city{Toulouse}, \country{France}}}

\abstract{In Gaussian process emulation of computer experiments with qualitative (categorical) inputs, estimating the $L \times L$ cross-level correlation matrix becomes a computational bottleneck when the number of levels $L$ is large ($L \ge 15$), requiring $D(L) = \frac{1}{2}L(L-1)$ hyperparameters. Supervised dimension reduction based on Partial Least Squares (PLS) has emerged as an appealing strategy to compress this search onto a low-dimensional latent elliptope ($\ell \ll L$). However, the mathematical and spectral properties of categorical PLS transformations have remained unestablished. In particular, previous engineering heuristics conjectured that PLS reconstructions preserve positive definiteness---a property we mathematically disprove, showing that unregularized reconstructions fail positive definiteness in up to $88.6\%$ of trials due to the dimensional contraction of the negative correlation boundary $-1/(L-1) \to 0^-$. In software implementations, this failure was heuristically patched via un-normalized diagonal nuggets that distort process variance, while exact alternating projections (Higham's algorithm) incur prohibitive $O(L^3)$ eigensolvers at every profile likelihood step.
In this paper, we establish the matrix-theoretic foundations of categorical PLS transformations on the correlation elliptope. We formalize a Row-Normalized PLS (RN-PLS) architecture ensuring bounded entries in $[-1, 1]$, establish the necessary rank and dimension conditions ($\ell \ge 3$) for non-trivial supervised reduction, and prove that the round-trip composite operator acts as a row-stochastic Markov transition operator with exact equicorrelation fixed points and Dobrushin dispersion non-expansion (with geometric contraction under scrambling or primitivity). We derive Gershgorin sufficient conditions revealing a sharp compression-reconstruction stability asymmetry, and prove that the admissible latent parameter domain is an open, bounded, convex set whose closure is a spectrahedron with zero disconnected feasibility pockets. To eliminate runtime eigensolvers, we introduce a closed-form diagonal shift-and-scaling repair operator that restores positive definiteness while preserving exact unit diagonals in $O(L^2)$ flops. Furthermore, we derive its analytical Jacobian across active polyhedral chambers, enabling quasi-Newton (L-BFGS-B) optimization with $O(\ell^2 L^2)$ Jacobian tensor assembly without finite differences. Finally, we disprove naive entrywise kernel exponentiation and construct a valid Hilbertian chordal distance kernel satisfying Schoenberg's theorem. Across multi-function benchmarks, our $O(L^2)$ repair reduces covariance condition numbers by $92.0\%$ and runtime by $73.8\%$ while preserving emulation accuracy.}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Computer experiments in modern engineering, computational physics, and applied statistics frequently involve a mixture of quantitative (continuous) and qualitative (categorical) inputs \citep{PDUDE,Deng,Zhou,zhang2020latent,DACE_hier,Roustant,Pelamatti,cuesta2021comparison,CAT-EGO,carpintero2025reproducible}. Representative applications include aerostructural design optimization across discrete wing configurations \citep{saves2023high}, materials selection among discrete alloy compositions \citep{audet2022general,Mixed_Abramson,AuHa2017}, and surrogate-based calibration across alternative turbulence closures or numerical solvers \citep{Pelamatti2020,Gower}.

Gaussian process (GP) metamodeling, or kriging, provides a Bayesian framework for emulating expensive deterministic computer codes with rigorous uncertainty quantification \citep{DACE,williams2006gaussian,Kennedy_Bayes,Jones98}. When the input space includes a qualitative factor $c \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ with $L$ unordered discrete levels, standard continuous Euclidean distance metrics fail. Following established formulations in spatial statistics and computer experiments \citep{PDUDE,Deng,Zhou,zhang2020latent,Roustant,Pelamatti}, cross-level statistical dependencies are modeled through a dedicated correlation matrix $\Theta \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L}$ constrained to the correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_L$ (symmetric, unit diagonal, and strictly positive definite). In standard unconstrained parameterizations, estimating $\Theta$ requires estimating $D(L) = \frac{1}{2}L(L-1)$ independent off-diagonal hyperparameters.

When the number of discrete levels $L$ is large (e.g., $L = 20 \implies D(20) = 190$ correlation parameters; or $L = 50 \implies D(50) = 1225$ parameters), estimating $\Theta$ via profile Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) or Cross-Validation becomes a severe computational bottleneck. The log-likelihood landscape is highly non-convex, multimodal, and numerically ill-conditioned. In an extensive comparative study of existing categorical covariance formulations, \citet{carpintero2025reproducible} systematically evaluated standard categorical kernels, demonstrating that without appropriate low-dimensional structure or grouping, estimating qualitative correlations becomes severely ill-conditioned as $L$ scales, while also highlighting the widespread lack of reproducible code and standardized benchmark suites across the literature.

To circumvent the burden of high-dimensional non-convex optimization, supervised dimension reduction based on Partial Least Squares (PLS) regression \citep{wold_1975,hellandStructurePartialLeast1988,bouhlel_KPLSK,Bouhlel18} has emerged as an attractive alternative \citep{saves2023high,saves2023smt,zuhal2021dimensionality,PLS-based}. By encoding categorical training samples through cross-level indicator vectors, PLS extracts $D(\ell) = \frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell-1) \ll D(L)$ orthogonal latent components (with $\ell \ge 3$, e.g., $\ell \in \{3, 4\}$) that maximize empirical covariance with the simulation response $y$, projecting the hyperparameter space onto a low-dimensional $\ell \times \ell$ latent correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_\ell$, requiring the MLE optimizer to estimate only $D(\ell) = \frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell-1)$ parameters (e.g., $\ell = 3 \implies D(3) = 3$ parameters instead of $190$ for $L=20$).

Despite its empirical success in multidisciplinary engineering optimization \citep{saves2023high,Mixed_Paul}, the mathematical and statistical foundations of this PLS transformation have remained entirely unestablished. In its initial formulation, \citet[Section~3.2]{saves2023high} introduced categorical PLS as an application-driven engineering surrogate for aerostructural design, leaving open four fundamental matrix-theoretic and computational questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] \textit{Elliptope Invariance:} It was conjectured that if the latent matrix $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{C}_\ell$ is positive definite, the reconstructed matrix $\Theta = \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})$ would automatically remain positive definite. As we prove in this work, this conjecture was a sampling artifact of low dimensions ($L \le 5$); in higher dimensions ($L \ge 15$), unregularized reconstruction fails positive definiteness in up to $88.6\%$ of cases due to the geometric contraction of the negative correlation boundary $-1/(L-1) \to 0^-$.
    \item[(ii)] \textit{Ad-Hoc Nuggets vs. Regularization Bottlenecks:} In practical software implementations \citep{saves2023smt}, runtime eigenvalue violations were mitigated by heuristically adding un-normalized diagonal nuggets ($\Theta + \eta I_L$), which violates the unit diagonal of the correlation elliptope ($[\Theta]_{j, j} = 1 + \eta \neq 1$) and distorts the process variance. Conversely, the standard mathematical remedy---computing the nearest correlation matrix via alternating projections \citep{higham2002computing}---requires iterative $O(L^3)$ eigensolvers at every profile likelihood evaluation, destroying the computational benefit of PLS reduction.
    \item[(iii)] \textit{Kernel Admissibility and High-Dimensional EHH Adaptation:} In its initial conception \citep{saves2023high}, the categorical PLS heuristic was specifically motivated by adapting the Exponential Homoscedastic Hypersphere (EHH) model \citep{Mixed_Paul} to high-dimensional categorical problems by analogy with continuous exponential KPLS kernels. To enforce positive entries ($k(c, c') > 0$), cross-level dependencies were modeled via naive entrywise exponentiation $k(c, c') = \exp(-[\Theta]_{c, c'})$. However, entrywise positivity does not imply spectral positive definiteness: as we establish herein, whenever off-diagonal correlations are negative, naive exponentiation violates Cauchy--Schwarz and produces indefinite Gram matrices with negative eigenvalues.
    \item[(iv)] \textit{Latent Feasibility Geometry and Gradient Computation:} Does the non-linear preimage of the positive definite cone induce non-convex feasibility traps or disconnected islands in latent parameter space? And can the spectral repair operator be integrated into gradient-based profile likelihood solvers (such as quasi-Newton L-BFGS-B) without resorting to computationally expensive finite differences or suffering non-smooth optimization breakdowns?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions of this work.}
The objective of this paper is to establish the mathematical and spectral properties of categorical PLS correlation transformations and to provide an $O(L^2)$ regularization scheme compatible with gradient-based profile likelihood estimation.\footnote{The complete open-source verification suite, formal proof artifacts, and reproducible benchmark codes are openly available at \url{https://github.com/Paul-Saves/pls-categorical-formalization}.} Our main contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Matrix-Theoretic Foundations and Markov Invariance:} We formalize the Row-Normalized PLS (RN-PLS) architecture, proving that unit row normalization guarantees reconstructed off-diagonal correlations remain within $[-1, 1]$ as convex combinations of latent parameters (Lemma~\ref{lem:convex_bound}). We prove that the round-trip composite operator $\mathcal{P} = \mathcal{R}_W \circ \mathcal{T}_{G_*}$ acts as a row-stochastic Markov transition operator with exact equicorrelation fixed points and Dobrushin dispersion non-expansion (Theorem~\ref{th:roundtrip_contraction}). Using Gershgorin circle theory, we derive spectral bounds revealing an intrinsic compression--reconstruction stability asymmetry, and prove that the admissible latent set $\mathcal{A}_L = \mathcal{C}_\ell \cap \mathcal{T}_{G_*}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_L)$ is an open, bounded, convex set whose closure is a spectrahedron (Theorem~\ref{th:admissible_convexity}), ruling out disconnected feasibility pockets.
    \item \textbf{Fast $O(L^2)$ Spectral Repair and Analytical Jacobians:} We identify the mechanism behind the empirical nugget phenomenon, proving that the emergence of negative eigenvalues is governed by the dimensional contraction of the negative correlation boundary $-1/(L-1) \to 0^-$ as $L$ scales (Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral_bounds}). To replace the $O(L^3)$ alternating projection bottleneck of Higham's algorithm, we introduce a diagonal shift-and-scaling repair operator that restores positive definiteness while preserving unit diagonals in $O(L^2)$ flops with zero eigenvalue computations (Theorem~\ref{th:reg_spd}). We derive the exact directional Jacobian of this repair operator across active polyhedral chambers (Theorem~\ref{th:gershgorin_gradient}), enabling quasi-Newton (L-BFGS-B) profile likelihood optimization with $O(\ell^2 L^2)$ tensor assembly without finite differences.
    \item \textbf{Metric Geometry and Schoenberg Admissibility:} We disprove the naive entrywise correlation exponentiation heuristic through non-PSD counterexamples violating Cauchy--Schwarz (Example~\ref{ex:non_psd_counterexample}), and construct a conditionally negative definite squared Hilbertian chordal distance on the correlation elliptope, proving positive definiteness for radial categorical kernels via Schoenberg's characterization (Theorem~\ref{th:hilbert_kernel_psd}). Across multi-function Gaussian process benchmarks, our $O(L^2)$ repair reduces covariance condition numbers by $92.0\%$ and runtime by $73.8\%$ while preserving emulation fidelity.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Notation and Mathematical Conventions.}
Throughout this article, we adhere to the following mathematical conventions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Matrix Spaces and Cones:} $\mathcal{S}^n$ denotes the real vector space of symmetric $n \times n$ matrices. $\mathcal{S}_1^n := \{ A \in \mathcal{S}^n : [A]_{j, j} = 1 \; \forall j \in \{1, \ldots, n\} \}$ is the affine subspace of symmetric matrices with unit diagonals. $\mathcal{H}_n := \{ A \in \mathcal{S}_1^n : |[A]_{j, j'}| \le 1 \; \forall 1 \le j < j' \le n \}$ denotes the compact correlation hypercube. $\mathcal{C}_n := \{ A \in \mathcal{S}_1^n : A \succ 0 \}$ denotes the open, bounded, convex \textit{correlation elliptope}, where $A \succ 0$ (resp. $A \succeq 0$) indicates symmetric positive definiteness (resp. semidefiniteness).
    \item \textit{Spectral Quantities and Matrix Operators:} $\lambda_{\min}(A)$ and $\lambda_{\max}(A)$ denote the smallest and largest eigenvalues of $A \in \mathcal{S}^n$, and $\kappa_2(A) = \lambda_{\max}(A) / \lambda_{\min}(A)$ is its 2-norm condition number. $I_n$ denotes the $n \times n$ identity matrix, $\mathds{1}_n = (1, \ldots, 1)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the vector of all ones, and $e_m$ is the $m$-th canonical basis vector in $\mathbb{R}^D$. For two matrices $A, B$ of identical dimensions, $A \odot B$ denotes the entrywise Hadamard product with $[A \odot B]_{i, j} = [A]_{i, j} [B]_{i, j}$.
    \item \textit{Dimensions and Pairwise Indices:} For any integer $n \ge 2$, $\mathcal{I}_n := \{ (j, j') : 1 \le j < j' \le n \}$ denotes the set of upper-triangular index pairs with cardinality $D(n) := \frac{1}{2}n(n - 1)$. We define the shorthands $D_{\inp} := D(L) = \frac{1}{2}L(L - 1)$ for the full categorical correlation space and $D_{\outp} := D(\ell) = \frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell - 1)$ for the reduced latent PLS correlation space ($\ell \ll L$).
    \item \textit{Row-Normalized PLS (RN-PLS) Convention:} In the context of our categorical PLS dimension reduction, $G_* \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp} \times D_{\outp}}$ refers exclusively to the row-normalized rotation matrix satisfying $\|[G_*]_{r, \cdot}\|_2 = 1$ for all rows $r \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\inp}\}$, unless explicitly qualified as raw unconstrained PLS.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Methodological Positioning}
\label{sec:sota}

The challenge of incorporating qualitative factors into spatial and computer experiment models has generated a rich body of literature across statistics, machine learning, and engineering optimization. Here, we establish a formal taxonomy of existing categorical covariance architectures, review regularization strategies for correlation matrices, and position our proposed RN-PLS framework against the state of the art.

\subsection{Taxonomy of Categorical Covariance Architectures}

Existing methods for qualitative Gaussian process emulation can be categorized into five primary paradigms:

\paragraph{Full-Rank Angular and Cholesky Parameterizations.}
To circumvent the positive-definiteness constraint $\Theta \succ 0$ during numerical optimization, \citet{pinheiro_unconstrained_1996} and \citet{HS,HS_Jacobi} introduced unconstrained spherical coordinate representations for correlation matrices, later adapted to qualitative computer experiments by \citet{Roustant} and \citet{Pelamatti}. In these formulations, the correlation matrix $\Theta = L_c L_c^\top$ is parameterized through trigonometric products of angular coordinates. While this representation guarantees that $\Theta$ resides within the correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_L$ without requiring projection steps, it does not achieve any dimensionality reduction: the optimizer must still search over all $D(L) = \frac{1}{2}L(L-1)$ trigonometric angles. As demonstrated empirically by \citet{carpintero2025reproducible}, for moderate to large dimensions ($L \ge 10$), the angular likelihood landscape becomes severely non-convex, exhibiting flat saddle regions, high multimodality, and slow optimization convergence.

\paragraph{Structured Group Kernels and Algebraic Composition.}
To model qualitative variables with large numbers of levels (such as chemical elements or nuclear isotopes), \citet{Roustant} formalized the algebraic composition of mixed-variable kernels $R = R_{\mathrm{quant}} \odot R_{\mathrm{qual}}$, where $\odot \in \{+, \times, \mathrm{ANOVA}\}$ denotes any positive-definiteness preserving operator. Furthermore, \citet{Roustant} introduced parsimonious \textit{group kernels} by assuming that the $L$ categorical levels are partitioned into $g \ll L$ hierarchical groups of varying sizes, equivalent to a nested Bayesian linear model. In this formulation, covariances within blocks and between pairs of blocks are held constant, such that the positive definiteness of the full $L \times L$ matrix is mathematically equivalent to the positive definiteness of a smaller $g \times g$ matrix obtained by averaging each block. While this provides a certified dimension reduction when expert domain knowledge provides a natural group partition, it is inherently rigid: it cannot be applied when no natural grouping is known, nor can it discover data-driven continuous latent representations.

\paragraph{Latent Variable Gaussian Processes (LVGP).}
Rather than parameterizing matrix entries directly, the Latent Variable Gaussian Process (LVGP) framework of \citet{zhang2020latent} maps each discrete categorical level $j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ to a continuous coordinate vector $z_j \in \mathbb{R}^k$ in a low-dimensional latent space ($k \in \{1, 2\}$). Inter-category correlations are then evaluated through a standard continuous stationary kernel, such as the Gaussian kernel $R_{\mathrm{qual}}(j, j') = \exp(-\|z_j - z_{j'}\|_2^2)$. By construction, LVGP guarantees positive semidefiniteness. However, the latent coordinates $\{z_j\}_{j=1}^L$ constitute $k \times L$ continuous parameters that must be estimated simultaneously with the continuous lengthscales via profile MLE. Moreover, LVGP is an \textit{unsupervised} embedding with respect to the initial dimension reduction: the latent coordinates are chosen during profile likelihood optimization rather than extracted a priori from empirical covariance with the simulation response $y$. Consequently, for large $L$, the optimization problem is high-dimensional, highly non-convex, and sensitive to numerical initialization \citep{oune2021latent,carpintero2025reproducible}.

\paragraph{Exchangeable and Distance-Based Formulations.}
At the opposite extreme of parameter complexity, the compound symmetry (exchangeable) model assumes a constant correlation $[\Theta]_{j, j'} = \rho$ for all $j \neq j'$. While this requires estimating only a single scalar parameter $\rho \in (-1/(L-1), 1)$, it imposes an inflexible symmetry that cannot capture heterogeneous cross-level interactions. Other approaches rely on fixed distance metrics between categorical levels, such as the Gower distance or Hamming distance \citep{PDUDE,Deng,Zhou}, but these lack the flexibility to adapt to complex response topographies. To introduce data-driven flexibility, early engineering formulations explored the Exponential Homoscedastic Hypersphere (EHH) model \citep{Mixed_Paul,saves2023high}, which attempted to enforce positive cross-level correlations via entrywise exponentiation $k(c, c') = \exp(-[\Theta]_{c, c'})$. However, as we establish analytically in Section~\ref{sec:kernels}, entrywise exponentiation lacks mathematical validity on the elliptope: whenever off-diagonal entries are negative, it violates Cauchy--Schwarz and produces indefinite Gram matrices with negative eigenvalues.

\paragraph{Supervised Dimension Reduction via Partial Least Squares.}
To overcome the limitations of unsupervised latent mappings and high-dimensional angular searches, supervised dimension reduction via Partial Least Squares (PLS) \citep{wold_1975,hellandStructurePartialLeast1988} has achieved widespread success in continuous computer experiments (KPLS) \citep{bouhlel_KPLSK,Bouhlel18}. In the context of qualitative factors, \citet{saves2023high} introduced an empirical cross-level indicator relaxation to project the $D(L)$-dimensional correlation space onto a low-dimensional latent elliptope $\mathcal{C}_\ell$ ($\ell \ll L$). Because the latent components maximize empirical covariance with the simulation response $y$, PLS focuses the optimization search on the most influential directions. However, this engineering formulation lacked mathematical foundations: the preservation of valid correlation bounds was unproven, the reconstructed matrices frequently failed positive definiteness, and software implementations resorted to ad-hoc un-normalized diagonal nuggets \citep{saves2023smt}.

\subsection{Spectral Repair and Regularization in Covariance Estimation}

When a candidate or reconstructed correlation matrix $\Theta$ violates positive definiteness ($\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) \le 0$), a repair or regularization mechanism is required to restore spectral validity.

\paragraph{Higham's Nearest Correlation Matrix.}
The standard mathematical approach for finding the nearest positive semidefinite correlation matrix in Frobenius norm was formulated by \citet{higham2002computing}. Higham's algorithm employs alternating Dykstra projections between two convex sets: the positive semidefinite cone $\mathcal{S}_+^L$ and the affine subspace of unit-diagonal matrices $\mathcal{S}_1^L$. While Higham's projection is mathematically exact, projecting onto $\mathcal{S}_+^L$ requires a full spectral decomposition of an $L \times L$ matrix ($O(L^3)$ flops) at every projection step, typically requiring dozens of iterations to converge. In profile likelihood optimization, where the qualitative correlation matrix must be evaluated and reconstructed at each quasi-Newton step, computing Higham projections introduces a severe computational bottleneck that completely negates the runtime benefits of PLS dimension reduction.

\paragraph{Linear Shrinkage and Gershgorin Localization.}
In sample covariance estimation, \citet{ledoit2004well} introduced an optimal linear shrinkage operator $\Theta^* = \alpha \Theta + (1-\alpha) I_L$ that minimizes expected Frobenius loss toward a spherical target. However, Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage theory is fundamentally asymptotic and statistical: the optimal intensity $\alpha^*$ is derived under large-sample random matrix limits ($n, p \to \infty$) from sample replicates. In deterministic computer experiments and Gaussian process metamodeling, $\Theta$ is not a sample covariance matrix computed from independent realizations, but a parametric cross-level correlation matrix evaluated at candidate hyperparameters.
To establish an exact, non-asymptotic repair operator suitable for deterministic kriging, we turn instead to matrix spectral localization theory \citep{horn2012matrix}. By bounding the eigenvalues of $\Theta$ via its absolute off-diagonal row sums, the Gershgorin circle theorem provides a direct, closed-form lower bound on $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta)$. As established in Section~\ref{sec:theorems}, this enables a deterministic diagonal shift-and-scaling repair operator that restores positive definiteness while preserving unit diagonals in $O(L^2)$ flops, requiring zero eigenvalue solvers and admitting exact analytical gradients.

\section{Statistical Framework and the RN-PLS Architecture}
\label{sec:setting}
\label{sec:operators}

\subsection{Qualitative Gaussian Process Emulation and the Correlation Elliptope}

Let $x \in \mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ denote a vector of continuous quantitative inputs, and let $c \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ denote a qualitative input with $L$ discrete levels. A computer experiment evaluates an expensive deterministic output $y(x, c) \in \mathbb{R}$. Under the Gaussian process emulation framework \citep{DACE,williams2006gaussian,Kennedy_Bayes,PDUDE}, $y(x, c)$ is modeled as a realization of a random field:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gp_model}
Y(x, c) = \mu(x, c) + Z(x, c),
\end{equation}
where $\mu(x, c)$ is a deterministic trend function (often assumed constant $\mu$), and $Z(x, c)$ is a zero-mean stationary Gaussian process with variance $\sigma^2$ and correlation function $R\bigl((x, c), (x', c')\bigr)$.

Following the algebraic characterization of \citet{Roustant}, joint mixed-variable covariance functions can be formulated via general composition operators $R\bigl((x, c), (x', c')\bigr) = R_{\mathrm{quant}}(x, x') \odot R_{\mathrm{qual}}(c, c')$, where $\odot$ is any algebraic operation preserving positive definiteness (such as addition $+$, tensor multiplication $\times$, or functional ANOVA decompositions). In this work, we deliberately adopt the separable multiplicative structure ($\odot = \times$, corresponding to the Hadamard entrywise product on sample Gram matrices):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:separable_kernel}
R\bigl((x, c), (x', c')\bigr) = R_{\mathrm{quant}}(x, x') \cdot R_{\mathrm{qual}}(c, c').
\end{equation}
The choice of the multiplicative operator ($\odot = \times$) is governed by four structural properties:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] \textit{Preservation of the Correlation Interval:} The real interval $[-1, 1]$ is closed under multiplication ($[-1, 1] \times [-1, 1] \subseteq [-1, 1]$). Consequently, multiplying valid correlation coefficients directly yields a valid correlation coefficient without requiring empirical scaling or artificial bounding.
    \item[(ii)] \textit{Preservation of Unit Diagonals:} For identical inputs, $R\bigl((x, c), (x, c)\bigr) = R_{\mathrm{quant}}(x, x) \cdot R_{\mathrm{qual}}(c, c) = 1 \cdot 1 = 1$, preserving the unit diagonal of the correlation elliptope without requiring variance re-weighting or normalizations.
    \item[(iii)] \textit{Homoscedastic Scale Factorization:} The global process variance $\sigma^2$ factors out homoscedastically as $\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(Y(x, c), Y(x', c')\bigr) = \sigma^2 R_{\mathrm{quant}}(x, x') R_{\mathrm{qual}}(c, c')$, enabling concentrated profile Maximum Likelihood Estimation over a single scalar variance parameter.
    \item[(iv)] \textit{Schur Product Positive Definiteness:} By the Schur product theorem \citep[Theorem~7.5.1]{horn2012matrix}, if $R_{\mathrm{quant}}$ and $R_{\mathrm{qual}}$ are positive semidefinite, their Hadamard product $R = R_{\mathrm{quant}} \odot R_{\mathrm{qual}}$ is positive semidefinite.
\end{enumerate}
While additive ($\odot = +$) or ANOVA structures represent valuable alternatives for isolating continuous and categorical variance components \citep{Deng,Roustant}, they require estimating separate variance parameters $(\sigma_{\mathrm{quant}}^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{qual}}^2)$ and depart from the geometry of the correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_L$. In Section~\ref{sec:conclu}, we discuss the generalization of our PLS dimension reduction and spectral repair theorems to such additive and heteroscedastic settings.

For the quantitative inputs, $R_{\mathrm{quant}}$ is typically chosen as a Mat\'ern or squared-exponential kernel parameterized by lengthscales $\bm{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^d$. For the qualitative input, cross-level dependencies are modeled through a dedicated correlation matrix $\Theta \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qual_kernel}
R_{\mathrm{qual}}(c, c') = [\Theta]_{c, c'},
\end{equation}
where $[\Theta]_{c, c'}$ denotes the correlation between category level $c$ and level $c'$.

To ensure that the Gaussian process $Y(x, c)$ is valid and well-defined, the correlation function must be positive definite. For the categorical component, this requires that $\Theta$ belongs to the correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_L$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:corr_def}
\Theta = \Theta^\top, \quad [\Theta]_{j, j} = 1 \quad \forall j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}, \quad \text{and} \quad \Theta \succ 0,
\end{equation}
where $\Theta \succ 0$ denotes Symmetric Positive Definiteness (SPD).

Geometrically, let $\mathcal{S}^n$ denote the vector space of real symmetric $n \times n$ matrices. We define the affine subspace of symmetric matrices with unit diagonals by:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:S1_def}
\mathcal{S}_1^n := \left\{ A \in \mathcal{S}^n : [A]_{j, j} = 1 \quad \forall j \in \{1, \ldots, n\} \right\},
\end{equation}
and the compact correlation hypercube by:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Hn_def}
\mathcal{H}_n := \left\{ A \in \mathcal{S}_1^n : |[A]_{j, j'}| \le 1 \quad \forall 1 \le j < j' \le n \right\}.
\end{equation}
The \textit{correlation elliptope} $\mathcal{C}_n$ is the open bounded convex subset of strictly positive definite matrices:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:elliptope_def}
\mathcal{C}_n := \left\{ A \in \mathcal{S}_1^n : A \succ 0 \right\} \subset \mathcal{H}_n \subset \mathcal{S}_1^n.
\end{equation}
The free degrees of freedom reside in the upper-triangular index pairs:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:I_L}
\mathcal{I}_L = \{ (j, j') \in \{1, \ldots, L\}^2 : j < j' \}, \quad |\mathcal{I}_L| = D_{\inp} := \frac{1}{2}L(L - 1).
\end{equation}
The correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_L$ is a bounded, convex spectrahedron whose topological boundary $\partial \mathcal{C}_L = \{ A \in \mathcal{S}_1^L : A \succeq 0, \, \det(A) = 0 \}$ consists of singular positive semidefinite matrices. As $L$ increases, the relative volume of $\mathcal{C}_L$ inside the hypercube $\mathcal{H}_L$ decays superexponentially \citep{Roustant,Pelamatti}, while the number of free hyperparameters $D_{\inp}$ grows quadratically.

To circumvent this curse of dimensionality, supervised dimension reduction aims to compress the high-dimensional parameter space $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp}}$ onto a low-dimensional latent elliptope $\mathcal{C}_\ell$ with $\ell \ll L$, depending on only:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:D_out}
D_{\outp} := \frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell - 1) \ll D_{\inp}
\end{equation}
hyperparameters (e.g., $D(3) = 3$ hyperparameters instead of $D(20) = 190$). However, because the correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_L$ is a curved, non-polyhedral manifold, linear transformations between $\mathcal{S}_1^\ell$ and $\mathcal{S}_1^L$ do not automatically preserve positive definiteness. This motivates the formal construction of the transformation operators below and their spectral characterization in Section~\ref{sec:theorems}.


\subsection{Cross-Level Indicator Encoding and Index Bijection}

To link discrete level observations to the pairwise correlation parameter space $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp}}$, we build upon the cross-level indicator relaxation originally introduced in an engineering context by \citet{saves2023high}, and establish its formal matrix-theoretic and algebraic properties.

\begin{definition}[Lexicographical Index Bijection $\psi$]
\label{def:psi}
For any integer $n \ge 2$, let $\mathcal{I}_n = \{ (k, k') : 1 \le k < k' \le n \}$. The lexicographical mapping $\psi(\cdot, \cdot, n) : \mathcal{I}_n \to \{1, \ldots, D(n)\}$ is defined by:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:psi_formula}
\psi(k, k', n) = \frac{1}{2}\left( (n-1)(n-2) - (n-k)(n-k-1) \right) + k' - 1.
\end{equation}
The mapping $\psi(\cdot, \cdot, n)$ is a bijection enumerating the upper-triangular pairs row by row. Its inverse is denoted $\psi^{-1}(\cdot, n) : \{1, \ldots, D(n)\} \to \mathcal{I}_n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cross-Level Indicator Relaxation]
\label{def:cross_level}
For an observed categorical level $c \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$, the cross-level indicator vector $\zeta_c \in \{0, 1\}^{D_{\inp}}$ is defined coordinate-wise for each $m \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\inp}\}$ by:
\begin{equation}
[\zeta_c]_m =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } j = c \text{ or } j' = c, \text{ where } (j, j') = \psi^{-1}(m, L), \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The vector $\zeta_c$ contains exactly $L-1$ ones and $D_{\inp} - (L - 1)$ zeros, indicating all pairwise correlations in which level $c$ participates.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Hadamard Coordinate Selection]
\label{lem:hadamard}
Let $c, c' \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ with $c \neq c'$. The Hadamard (element-wise) product $\zeta_c \odot \zeta_{c'} \in \{0, 1\}^{D_{\inp}}$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hadamard_select}
[\zeta_c \odot \zeta_{c'}]_m =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } m = \psi(\min(c, c'),\, \max(c, c'),\, L), \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\zeta_c \odot \zeta_{c'} = e_{m^*}$, the canonical basis vector in $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp}}$ associated with the pair $\{c, c'\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Definition~\ref{def:cross_level}: for $c \neq c'$, $\{c, c'\}$ is the unique unordered pair containing both categorical levels, indexed bijectively by $m^* = \psi(\min(c, c'), \max(c, c'), L)$, while all other coordinates vanish.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Bidirectional Transformation Operators}

\begin{proposition}[Incidence Gram Identity, Exact Rank, and Krylov Degeneracy]
\label{prop:rank_bound}
Let $c_1, \ldots, c_{n_t} \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ with $L \ge 3$ be $n_t$ categorical observations with one-hot assignment matrix $M \in \{0, 1\}^{n_t \times L}$ ($L_{\mathrm{obs}}$ observed levels). Let $E \in \{0, 1\}^{L \times D_{\inp}}$ denote the unoriented vertex-edge incidence matrix of the complete graph $K_L$, whose $j$-th row is the cross-level indicator vector $\zeta_j^\top$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Incidence Gram Identity and Isometry on $\mathds{1}_L^\perp$:} The design matrix factors as $X = M E \in \mathbb{R}^{n_t \times D_{\inp}}$, and the incidence matrix satisfies the exact algebraic identity:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:incidence_gram}
    E E^\top = (L - 2) I_L + \mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top.
    \end{equation}
    Its spectrum consists of $\lambda_1(E E^\top) = 2(L - 1)$ (multiplicity $1$, eigenvector $\mathds{1}_L$) and $\lambda_2 = \cdots = \lambda_L = L - 2$ on the centered subspace $\mathds{1}_L^\perp$. Consequently, for any $u \in \mathds{1}_L^\perp$, $E^\top$ is an exact homothety with $\|E^\top u\|_2 = \sqrt{L - 2} \|u\|_2$, establishing $\operatorname{rank}(E) = L$.
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Exact Column Rank of Centered Design $\tilde{X}$:} Centering $X$ via $H_{n_t} = I_{n_t} - \frac{1}{n_t}\mathds{1}_{n_t}\mathds{1}_{n_t}^\top$ yields $\tilde{X} = H_{n_t} M E$, whose covariance Gram matrix satisfies:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:centered_gram_factor}
    \tilde{X} \tilde{X}^\top = (L - 2) H_{n_t} M M^\top H_{n_t},
    \end{equation}
    with exact column rank $\operatorname{rank}(\tilde{X}) = L_{\mathrm{obs}} - 1 \le \min(n_t - 1, L - 1)$.
    \item[(c)] \textbf{Krylov Subspace Degeneracy in Pure Balanced Designs:} When observations are purely categorical and balanced ($n_j = n_t / L$ for all $j$), $\tilde{X}\tilde{X}^\top = \frac{n_t}{L}(L - 2) M H_L M^\top$ has a single distinct non-zero eigenvalue $\frac{n_t}{L}(L - 2)$ of multiplicity $L - 1$. Consequently, the univariate PLS Krylov subspace $\mathcal{K}_m(\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X}, \tilde{X}^\top \tilde{\bm{y}})$ is 1-dimensional, terminating in a single step ($D_{\outp} = 1$). To extract $D_{\outp} = \frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell-1) \ge 2$ informative directions without Krylov breakdown ($D_{\outp} \le L_{\mathrm{obs}} - 1$), PLS requires coupling with continuous covariates (as in mixed KPLS) or hierarchical categorical groupings.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For (a), row $j$ of $E$ has entry $1$ for each of the $L-1$ edges incident to vertex $j$, so $[E E^\top]_{j, j} = L - 1$. For $j \neq j'$, the pair $\{j, j'\}$ forms a unique edge in $K_L$, so $[E E^\top]_{j, j'} = 1$. Thus $E E^\top = (L - 1)I_L + (\mathds{1}_L\mathds{1}_L^\top - I_L) = (L - 2)I_L + \mathds{1}_L\mathds{1}_L^\top$. For $u \perp \mathds{1}_L$, $E E^\top u = (L - 2)u$, so $\|E^\top u\|_2^2 = (L - 2)\|u\|_2^2$. For $L \ge 3$, $L - 2 > 0$ and $2(L - 1) > 0$, proving $\operatorname{rank}(E) = L$. For (b), $X = M E$ implies $\tilde{X}\tilde{X}^\top = H_{n_t} M E E^\top M^\top H_{n_t}$. Since $M \mathds{1}_L = \mathds{1}_{n_t}$ and $H_{n_t} \mathds{1}_{n_t} = \bm{0}$, the term $\mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top$ vanishes, establishing \eqnref{eq:centered_gram_factor}. Since $E$ has full row rank $L$, $\operatorname{rank}(\tilde{X}) = \operatorname{rank}(H_{n_t} M) = L_{\mathrm{obs}} - 1$. For (c), in balanced designs $M^\top M = \frac{n_t}{L} I_L$, deflating residual Krylov directions $m \ge 2$. Complete algebraic details are in Appendix~\ref{subsec:proof_rank_bound}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Resolution of Krylov Degeneracy via Joint Continuous--Categorical PLS.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_bound}(c) establishes that in an uncoupled, purely categorical balanced design with no continuous variation, the covariance operator collapses onto a single non-trivial eigenspace, rendering higher-order categorical Krylov directions uninformative. In mixed-variable Gaussian process emulation (as implemented in the Surrogate Modeling Toolbox and evaluated in our benchmarks), this degeneracy is naturally eliminated: PLS regression is performed on the joint design matrix:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:joint_pls_matrix}
X_{\mathrm{joint}} = \begin{bmatrix} X_{\mathrm{quant}} & X_{\mathrm{cat}} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_t \times (d + D_{\inp})}.
\end{equation}
The continuous inputs $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ couple with the response variation across distinct categorical levels, breaking the homothety of $X_{\mathrm{cat}}^\top X_{\mathrm{cat}}$. Specifically, because the global Latin Hypercube design ($n_j = 3$ per level) induces non-zero sample cross-covariances $\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{cat}}^\top \tilde{X}_{\mathrm{quant}} \neq \bm{0}$ across levels and empirical standardization scales each continuous and categorical column by its empirical variance, the joint covariance $\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}^\top \tilde{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}$ breaks the categorical eigenspace degeneracy. Because $\operatorname{rank}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{cat}}) = L_{\mathrm{obs}} - 1$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_bound}(b), concatenating $d$ continuous design columns yields the sharp rank bound:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:krylov_bound}
\dim \mathcal{K}_m(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}^\top \tilde{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}, \tilde{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}^\top \tilde{\bm{y}}) \le \operatorname{rank}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}) \le \min(n_t - 1, d + L_{\mathrm{obs}} - 1).
\end{equation}
Equality holds generically whenever the continuous design columns are linearly independent, the centered joint covariance matrix $\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}^\top \tilde{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}$ possesses mutually distinct non-zero eigenvalues, and the initial PLS projection vector $\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}^\top \tilde{\bm{y}}$ is not orthogonal to any invariant eigenspace. To ensure that $D_{\outp} = \frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell-1)$ non-degenerate latent directions can be extracted without Krylov rank starvation, the mixed problem must satisfy $d + L_{\mathrm{obs}} - 1 \ge D_{\outp}$. In our multi-material benchmarks ($n_t = 36 - 60, d \ge 3, L \ge 12$), continuous and categorical columns are standardized prior to decomposition, and NIPALS PLS extracts $D_{\outp} = 3$ non-degenerate orthogonal components with non-vanishing weight norms ($\|w_k\| \in \{37.5, 4.32, 7.37\}$), fully eliminating the 1-dimensional Krylov collapse. From the joint rotation matrix $G_{\mathrm{joint}} = W_{\mathrm{joint}} (P_{\mathrm{joint}}^\top W_{\mathrm{joint}})^{-1}$, the categorical submatrix $G_{\mathrm{cat}} = [G_{\mathrm{joint}}]_{d+1:d+D_{\inp}, \cdot} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp} \times D_{\outp}}$ is extracted and row-normalized to yield the valid unit-row-norm reconstruction weights $G_* \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp} \times D_{\outp}}$ for all $D_{\outp} = \frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell-1)$ latent dimensions.

\begin{definition}[Reconstruction Operator $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}$]
\label{def:operator}
Let $G_* \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp} \times D_{\outp}}$. The reconstruction operator $\mathcal{T}_{G_*} : \mathcal{S}_1^\ell \to \mathcal{S}_1^L$ maps a reduced symmetric matrix $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{S}_1^\ell$ to an $L \times L$ symmetric matrix $\Theta = \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}) \in \mathcal{S}_1^L$ defined by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Unit diagonal: $[\Theta]_{j, j} = 1$ for all $j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$;
    \item Off-diagonal entries: for all $1 \le j < j' \le L$,
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:recon_formula}
    [\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})]_{j, j'} = \sum_{t=1}^{\ell} \sum_{t'=t+1}^{\ell} \left( [G_*]_{\psi(j,j',L), \psi(t,t',\ell)} \right)^2 [\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'};
    \end{equation}
    \item Symmetry: $[\Theta]_{j', j} = [\Theta]_{j, j'}$ for all $1 \le j < j' \le L$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reduction Operator $\mathcal{R}_W$]
\label{def:reduction_op}
Let $W \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp} \times D_{\inp}}$ be a non-negative row-stochastic matrix, meaning that $[W]_{p, r} \ge 0$ for all $p \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\outp}\}$ and $r \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\inp}\}$, with row sums satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:row_stochastic}
\sum_{r=1}^{D_{\inp}} [W]_{p, r} = 1, \quad \forall p \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\outp}\}.
\end{equation}
The reduction operator $\mathcal{R}_W : \mathcal{S}_1^L \to \mathcal{S}_1^\ell$ maps an $L \times L$ symmetric matrix $\Theta \in \mathcal{S}_1^L$ to an $\ell \times \ell$ symmetric matrix $\hat{\Theta} = \mathcal{R}_W(\Theta) \in \mathcal{S}_1^\ell$ defined by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Unit diagonal: $[\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t} = 1$ for all $t \in \{1, \ldots, \ell\}$;
    \item Off-diagonal entries: for all $1 \le t < t' \le \ell$, setting row index $p = \psi(t, t', \ell) \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\outp}\}$,
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:reduc_formula}
    [\mathcal{R}_W(\Theta)]_{t, t'} = \sum_{j=1}^L \sum_{j'=j+1}^L [W]_{p, \psi(j,j',L)} \, [\Theta]_{j, j'};
    \end{equation}
    \item Symmetry: $[\hat{\Theta}]_{t', t} = [\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'}$ for all $1 \le t < t' \le \ell$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Computational and Statistical Utility of the Reduction Operator]
\label{rem:reduction_utility}
While Gaussian process emulation primarily executes the reconstruction operator $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}$ to map latent parameters into the full correlation space during inference, the adjoint reduction operator $\mathcal{R}_W$ provides a practical computational capability: it enables an instantaneous, closed-form projection to \textit{warm-start} profile likelihood optimization. Specifically, if a full-space prior or empirical correlation matrix $\Theta_{\mathrm{prior}}$ is available (e.g., from physical domain knowledge, nominal configurations, or exploratory inter-level empirical response correlations $\Theta_{\mathrm{emp}}$), setting $\hat{\Theta}_0 = \mathcal{R}_W(\Theta_{\mathrm{prior}})$ provides an admissible, well-conditioned starting point in $\mathcal{C}_\ell$, bypassing computationally expensive non-convex Multidimensional Scaling (MDS) procedures. As demonstrated empirically in Figure~\ref{fig:repair_tradeoff}, this adjoint warm-start initiates quasi-Newton trajectories in immediate proximity to the global optimum, reducing L-BFGS-B iterations by a factor of 3 and accelerating convergence relative to random multi-starts.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Unit Row-Norm Condition and the Row-Normalized PLS (RN-PLS) Model}

\begin{assumption}[Unit Row-Norm Property]
\label{ass:unit_row_norm}
The PLS rotation matrix $G_* \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp} \times D_{\outp}}$ satisfies: for every row index $r \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\inp}\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unit_row_norm}
\omega_r \;:=\; \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} \left( [G_*]_{r, p} \right)^2 = 1.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, every row vector of $G_*$ has unit Euclidean norm: $\|[G_*]_{r, \cdot}\|_2 = 1$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[Statistical Qualification of the RN-PLS Architecture and Canonical Adjoint]
\label{rem:enforce}
In standard, unconstrained PLS regression, the rotation matrix $G_*^{\mathrm{raw}} = G(\Xi^\top G)^{-1}$ does not possess unit row norms in general \citep{wold_1975,hellandStructurePartialLeast1988,bouhlel_KPLSK,Bouhlel18}. As shown in our empirical simulations (Section~\ref{sec:simulations}), row norms $\sqrt{\omega_r}$ in raw PLS can vary widely (e.g., from $0.01$ to $4.9$ for $L=20$), causing un-normalized reconstruction weights $\sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*^{\mathrm{raw}}]_{r,p})^2$ to fluctuate by up to two orders of magnitude and driving reconstructed correlations far outside $[-1, 1]$.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:row_normalization}
[G_*]_{r, p} =
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{[G_*^{\mathrm{raw}}]_{r, p}}{\sqrt{\omega_r}}, & \text{if } \omega_r = \sum_{p'=1}^{D_{\outp}} \left([G_*^{\mathrm{raw}}]_{r, p'}\right)^2 > 0, \\[8pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{D_{\outp}}}, & \text{if } \omega_r = 0,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where the convention for $\omega_r = 0$ handles the uninformative boundary case when a categorical pair is unobserved in the training sample, assigning equal prior weight across latent coordinates while strictly preserving the unit Euclidean norm $\|[G_*]_{r, \cdot}\|_2 = 1$.
Importantly, row normalization applies non-uniform scalings across different pairs $\{j, j'\}$, reweighting the relative importance of each pairwise correlation coordinate. RN-PLS should therefore be understood as a structurally constrained projection architecture designed specifically to guarantee convex combination weights on the correlation elliptope, rather than as an exact reparameterization of unconstrained PLS.

Furthermore, whenever all $D_{\outp} = \frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell-1)$ latent components extracted by PLS are informative and active---meaning that every column of $G_*$ is non-zero, satisfying $\sum_{r'=1}^{D_{\inp}} ([G_*]_{r', p})^2 > 0$ for all $p \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\outp}\}$---the canonical adjoint row-stochastic reduction matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp} \times D_{\inp}}$ is given in closed form by:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W_definition}
[W]_{p, r} = \frac{\left([G_*]_{r, p}\right)^2}{\sum_{r'=1}^{D_{\inp}} \left([G_*]_{r', p}\right)^2}, \quad \forall p \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\outp}\}, \; r \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\inp}\},
\end{equation}
ensuring that $\sum_{r=1}^{D_{\inp}} [W]_{p, r} = 1$ and each reduced latent correlation is an exact convex combination of full-space correlations.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Convex Combination Weights and Hypercube Preservation]
\label{lem:convex}\label{lem:entry_bound}\label{th:entry_bound}\label{lem:convex_bound}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:unit_row_norm}, for any $(j, j') \in \mathcal{I}_L$, the reconstruction weights defined by:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weights}
w_{(j,j')}^{(t,t')} := \left( [G_*]_{\psi(j,j',L), \psi(t,t',\ell)} \right)^2, \quad \forall (t, t') \in \mathcal{I}_\ell,
\end{equation}
satisfy $w_{(j,j')}^{(t,t')} \ge 0$ and $\sum_{(t,t') \in \mathcal{I}_\ell} w_{(j,j')}^{(t,t')} = 1$. Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] Every off-diagonal entry $[\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})]_{j, j'}$ is a convex combination of the latent off-diagonal entries $\{ [\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'} : (t, t') \in \mathcal{I}_\ell \}$.
    \item[(b)] The operators preserve the correlation hypercube: $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\mathcal{H}_\ell) \subseteq \mathcal{H}_L$ and $\mathcal{R}_W(\mathcal{H}_L) \subseteq \mathcal{H}_\ell$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Non-negativity holds because each weight is the square of a real number. For (a), let $r = \psi(j, j', L)$. Because $\psi(\cdot, \cdot, \ell)$ is a bijection from $\mathcal{I}_\ell$ to $\{1, \ldots, D_{\outp}\}$, re-indexing yields $\sum_{(t,t')} w_{(j,j')}^{(t,t')} = \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*]_{r, p})^2 = \omega_r = 1$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:unit_row_norm}. For (b), by the triangle inequality, $|[\Theta]_{j, j'}| \le \sum_{(t,t')} w_{(j,j')}^{(t,t')} |[\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'}| \le \sum_{(t,t')} w_{(j,j')}^{(t,t')} \cdot 1 = 1$, with unit diagonals preserved by definition. The same convex combination bound applies to $\mathcal{R}_W$ via the row-stochasticity of $W$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Degeneracy of Row-Normalized PLS for Latent Dimension $\ell=2$]
\label{prop:l2_degeneracy}
Let Assumption~\ref{ass:unit_row_norm} hold with latent dimension $\ell = 2$, so that the latent correlation space has dimension $D_{\outp} = \frac{1}{2}(2)(1) = 1$. Suppose every row of $G_*^{\mathrm{raw}}$ is non-zero ($\omega_r > 0$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Trivial Row Weights:} For every coordinate $r \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\inp}\}$, the normalized rotation matrix satisfies:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:l2_weight_trivial}
    \left([G_*]_{r, 1}\right)^2 \equiv 1.
    \end{equation}
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Collapse to Equicorrelation (Compound Symmetry):} For any latent correlation matrix $\hat{\Theta} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \rho \\ \rho & 1 \end{pmatrix} \in \mathcal{C}_2$ parameterized by scalar $\rho \in (-1, 1)$, the reconstructed matrix $\Theta = \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}) \in \mathcal{S}_1^L$ collapses identically to the uniform equicorrelation matrix:
    \begin{equation}
    [\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})]_{j, j'} = \rho \quad \forall 1 \le j < j' \le L, \quad \text{i.e., } \Theta = (1 - \rho) I_L + \rho \mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top.
    \end{equation}
    \item[(c)] \textbf{Erasure of the PLS Projection:} The reconstructed matrix $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})$ is completely invariant to the cross-level design matrix $X$, the response vector $y$, and the raw PLS rotation matrix $G_*^{\mathrm{raw}}$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, for RN-PLS to perform non-trivial, data-driven differential weighting across correlation coordinates, a necessary mathematical condition is $D_{\outp} \ge 2$, which strictly requires $\ell \ge 3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For (a), when $\ell=2$, $D_{\outp}=1$, so $G_*^{\mathrm{raw}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp} \times 1}$ has a single column. The row norm is $\sqrt{\omega_r} = |[G_*^{\mathrm{raw}}]_{r, 1}|$. Dividing by $\sqrt{\omega_r}$ yields $[G_*]_{r, 1} = \operatorname{sgn}\bigl([G_*^{\mathrm{raw}}]_{r, 1}\bigr) \in \{-1, +1\}$, so $([G_*]_{r, 1})^2 \equiv 1$ for all $r \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\inp}\}$.
For (b), substituting $([G_*]_{r, 1})^2 = 1$ into Definition~\ref{def:operator} yields $[\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})]_{j, j'} = 1 \cdot [\hat{\Theta}]_{1, 2} = \rho$ for all $j < j'$.
For (c), because $([G_*]_{r, 1})^2 \equiv 1$ regardless of the numerical entries of $G_*^{\mathrm{raw}}$, all data information captured by PLS is identically canceled by the unit row normalization. To obtain non-trivial convex combination weights where $\sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*]_{r, p})^2 = 1$ with $[G_*]_{r, p}^2 \not\equiv 1$, we must have $D_{\outp} \ge 2 \iff \frac{1}{2}\ell(\ell-1) \ge 2 \iff \ell \ge 3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-Trip Adjoint Factorization, Detailed Balance, and Spectral Gap]
\label{th:roundtrip_contraction}
Let Assumption~\ref{ass:unit_row_norm} hold with active columns $d_p = \sum_{r=1}^{D_{\inp}} ([G_*]_{r, p})^2 > 0$ for all $p \in \{1, \dots, D_{\outp}\}$. Let $A := G_* \odot G_* \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{D_{\inp} \times D_{\outp}}$ denote the reconstruction weight matrix, $D_{\mathrm{col}} := \diag(d_1, \dots, d_{D_{\outp}})$, and let $W = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1} A^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp} \times D_{\inp}}$ be the canonical row-stochastic adjoint matrix defined in \eqnref{eq:W_definition}. Consider the round-trip composite operator $\mathcal{P} := \mathcal{R}_W \circ \mathcal{T}_{G_*} : \mathcal{S}_1^\ell \to \mathcal{S}_1^\ell$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Matrix Factorization, Reversibility, and Stationary Distribution:} For any $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{S}_1^\ell$ with off-diagonal vector $\hat{\bm{\theta}} = (\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{D_{\outp}})^\top$, the composite operator acts linearly on the off-diagonal entries:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:roundtrip_matrix}
    \operatorname{vec}_{\mathrm{off}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(\hat{\Theta})\bigr) = M \hat{\bm{\theta}}, \quad \text{where } M := W A = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1} A^\top A \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp} \times D_{\outp}}.
    \end{equation}
    The transition matrix $M$ is row-stochastic ($M \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}} = \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}}$) and reversible with respect to $D_{\mathrm{col}}$, satisfying detailed balance:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:detailed_balance}
    D_{\mathrm{col}} M = A^\top A = M^\top D_{\mathrm{col}}.
    \end{equation}
    A stationary distribution is given explicitly in closed form by:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:stationary_pi}
    \bm{\pi} = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \bm{d} = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} (d_1, \dots, d_{D_{\outp}})^\top \in \Delta_{D_{\outp}}, \quad \text{with } \bm{\pi}^\top M = \bm{\pi}^\top,
    \end{equation}
    which is unique whenever the Markov chain induced by $M$ is irreducible.
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Entirely Real Positive Semi-Definite Spectrum in $[0, 1]$:} The transition matrix $M$ is similar to the symmetric positive semi-definite matrix:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:sym_markov_S}
    S := D_{\mathrm{col}}^{1/2} M D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} A^\top A D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} = (A D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2})^\top (A D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2}) \succeq 0.
    \end{equation}
    Consequently, all $D_{\outp}$ eigenvalues of $M$ are strictly real, non-negative, and bounded by 1:
    \begin{equation}
    0 \le \lambda_{D_{\outp}}(M) \le \dots \le \lambda_2(M) \le \lambda_1(M) = 1.
    \end{equation}
    In particular, $M$ possesses no complex conjugate eigenvalues, no oscillatory modes, no defective Jordan blocks, and is diagonalizable in $\mathbb{R}$.
    \item[(c)] \textbf{Preservation of Reconstructed Full-Space Mean Correlation:} For any latent correlation vector $\hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$, the arithmetic mean of all $D_{\inp}$ reconstructed pairwise correlations $\bm{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}} = A \hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp}}$ satisfies:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mean_preservation}
    \bar{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}} := \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \sum_{r=1}^{D_{\inp}} [\bm{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}}]_r = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\inp}}^\top A \hat{\bm{\theta}} = \bm{\pi}^\top \hat{\bm{\theta}}.
    \end{equation}
    This reconstructed full-space mean is an exact invariant under the round-trip operator: $\bm{\pi}^\top (M \hat{\bm{\theta}}) = \bm{\pi}^\top \hat{\bm{\theta}} = \bar{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}}$.
    \item[(d)] \textbf{Geometric Convergence to Equicorrelation via Spectral Gap:} If the Markov chain induced by $M$ is irreducible, the second eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_2(M) < 1$. Under iterated round-trip projections $\mathcal{P}^k$, the latent correlations converge geometrically to the uniform equicorrelation matrix $\hat{\Theta}(\bar{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}})$:
    \begin{equation}
    \lim_{k \to \infty} M^k \hat{\bm{\theta}} = \bar{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}},
    \end{equation}
    with contraction rate governed by the spectral gap $1 - \lambda_2(M)$:
    \begin{equation}
    \|M^k \hat{\bm{\theta}} - \bar{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}}\|_{D_{\mathrm{col}}} \le \lambda_2(M)^k \|\hat{\bm{\theta}} - \bar{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}}\|_{D_{\mathrm{col}}},
    \end{equation}
    where $\|v\|_{D_{\mathrm{col}}} = \sqrt{v^\top D_{\mathrm{col}} v} = \sqrt{D_{\inp}} \|v\|_{\bm{\pi}}$.
    \item[(e)] \textbf{Dobrushin Ergodic Oscillation Non-Expansion:} For single-step coordinate dispersion $\operatorname{spread}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) := \max_p \hat{\theta}_p - \min_p \hat{\theta}_p$, the unconditional non-expansion bound holds:
    \begin{equation}
    \operatorname{spread}(M \hat{\bm{\theta}}) \le \tau(M) \operatorname{spread}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}),
    \end{equation}
    where $\tau(M) = 1 - \min_{p, p'} \sum_{q=1}^{D_{\outp}} \min([M]_{p, q}, [M]_{p', q}) = \frac{1}{2} \max_{p, p'} \sum_{q=1}^{D_{\outp}} |[M]_{p, q} - [M]_{p', q}| \le 1$ is Dobrushin's ergodic coefficient of $M$, with strict single-step contraction ($\tau(M) < 1$) holding if and only if $M$ satisfies Dobrushin's scrambling condition.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (a), by Definition~\ref{def:reduction_op} and Definition~\ref{def:operator}, for any $1 \le t < t' \le \ell$ with $p = \psi(t, t', \ell)$:
\begin{equation}
[\mathcal{P}(\hat{\Theta})]_{t, t'} = \sum_{r=1}^{D_{\inp}} [W]_{p, r} \sum_{q=1}^{D_{\outp}} A_{r, q} \hat{\theta}_q = \sum_{q=1}^{D_{\outp}} [W A]_{p, q} \hat{\theta}_q = [M \hat{\bm{\theta}}]_p.
\end{equation}
By \eqnref{eq:W_definition}, $[W]_{p, r} = A_{r, p} / d_p = [D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1} A^\top]_{p, r}$, so $W = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1} A^\top$ and $M = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1} A^\top A$.
Because $A \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}} = \mathds{1}_{D_{\inp}}$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:unit_row_norm}, we have $A^\top \mathds{1}_{D_{\inp}} = \bm{d} = D_{\mathrm{col}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}}$, which yields $M \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}} = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1} A^\top (A \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}}) = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1} A^\top \mathds{1}_{D_{\inp}} = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1} \bm{d} = \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}}$, proving row-stochasticity.
Multiplying $M$ by $D_{\mathrm{col}}$ gives $D_{\mathrm{col}} M = A^\top A = (D_{\mathrm{col}} M)^\top = M^\top D_{\mathrm{col}}$, which is the detailed balance condition.
Because $\sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} d_p = \sum_{r=1}^{D_{\inp}} \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} A_{r, p} = D_{\inp}$, the vector $\bm{\pi} = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}}\bm{d}$ lies in the probability simplex $\Delta_{D_{\outp}}$ and satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\bm{\pi}^\top M = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \bm{d}^\top (D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1} A^\top A) = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}}^\top A^\top A = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} (A \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}})^\top A = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\inp}}^\top A = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \bm{d}^\top = \bm{\pi}^\top.
\end{equation}

For (b), let $B = A D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp} \times D_{\outp}}$. Then $S = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{1/2} M D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} A^\top A D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} = B^\top B$.
As a Gram matrix of real vectors, $S$ is real, symmetric, and positive semi-definite ($u^\top S u = \|B u\|_2^2 \ge 0$).
Because $M$ and $S$ are related by a similarity transformation, their eigenvalues coincide identically: $\lambda_i(M) = \lambda_i(S) \ge 0$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, D_{\outp}\}$.
By the Perron--Frobenius theorem for row-stochastic matrices, the spectral radius satisfies $\rho(M) = 1$, which establishes $0 \le \lambda_{D_{\outp}}(M) \le \dots \le \lambda_1(M) = 1$.
Because $S$ is symmetric, it admits an orthonormal basis of real eigenvectors, proving that $M$ is diagonalizable in $\mathbb{R}$ with no defective Jordan blocks.

For (c), the arithmetic mean of the reconstructed full correlations is:
\begin{equation}
\bar{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}} = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \sum_{r=1}^{D_{\inp}} [A \hat{\bm{\theta}}]_r = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\inp}}^\top A \hat{\bm{\theta}} = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \bm{d}^\top \hat{\bm{\theta}} = \bm{\pi}^\top \hat{\bm{\theta}}.
\end{equation}
Using $\bm{\pi}^\top M = \bm{\pi}^\top$, we obtain $\bm{\pi}^\top (M \hat{\bm{\theta}}) = (\bm{\pi}^\top M) \hat{\bm{\theta}} = \bm{\pi}^\top \hat{\bm{\theta}} = \bar{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}}$, proving invariance.

For (d), let $S = \sum_{i=1}^{D_{\outp}} \lambda_i u_i u_i^\top$ be the spectral decomposition of $S$, where $u_1 = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{1/2} \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}} / \sqrt{D_{\inp}}$ satisfies $S u_1 = u_1$ with $\|u_1\|_2 = 1$.
Because $M = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} S D_{\mathrm{col}}^{1/2}$, powers of $M$ satisfy $M^k = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} S^k D_{\mathrm{col}}^{1/2}$.
Since $\lambda_1 = 1$ and $\lambda_i \le \lambda_2(M) < 1$ for all $i \ge 2$, $S^k = u_1 u_1^\top + O(\lambda_2(M)^k)$, which implies:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{k \to \infty} M^k = D_{\mathrm{col}}^{-1/2} (u_1 u_1^\top) D_{\mathrm{col}}^{1/2} = \frac{1}{D_{\inp}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}} \bm{d}^\top = \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}} \bm{\pi}^\top.
\end{equation}
Applying this to $\hat{\bm{\theta}}$ gives $\lim_{k \to \infty} M^k \hat{\bm{\theta}} = \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}} (\bm{\pi}^\top \hat{\bm{\theta}}) = \bar{\theta}_{\mathrm{full}} \mathds{1}_{D_{\outp}}$.
In the stationary-weighted inner product $\langle u, v \rangle_{D_{\mathrm{col}}} := u^\top D_{\mathrm{col}} v$, the operator norm of $M - \mathds{1}\bm{\pi}^\top$ is exactly the second eigenvalue of $S$: $\|M - \mathds{1}\bm{\pi}^\top\|_{D_{\mathrm{col}}} = \lambda_2(M)$, yielding the geometric contraction bound.

For (e), the oscillation bound follows from Dobrushin's classical ergodic coefficient theorem for Markov transition operators on the $\ell_\infty$ oscillation seminorm.
\end{proof}

\section{Theoretical Properties and Positive Definiteness Guarantees}
\label{sec:theorems}

\subsection{Gershgorin Bounds and Elliptope Preservation Asymmetry}
\label{subsec:hypercube_gershgorin}

While Lemma~\ref{lem:entry_bound} guarantees that $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}$ and $\mathcal{R}_W$ map between correlation hypercubes ($\mathcal{H}_\ell \to \mathcal{H}_L$ and $\mathcal{H}_L \to \mathcal{H}_\ell$), preserving positive definiteness---and thereby mapping between the open correlation elliptopes $\mathcal{C}_\ell$ and $\mathcal{C}_L$---requires spectral localization. The following theorem establishes sharp sufficient conditions under diagonal dominance.

\begin{theorem}[Sufficient Conditions for Elliptope Preservation under Gershgorin Dominance]
\label{th:spd_condition}
Let Assumption~\ref{ass:unit_row_norm} hold and let $W$ satisfy Definition~\ref{def:reduction_op}.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Reconstruction direction ($\mathcal{C}_\ell \to \mathcal{C}_L$):} Let $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{C}_\ell$. If the off-diagonal entries satisfy:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:recon_bound}
    \rho_{\max} := \max_{(t,t') \in \mathcal{I}_\ell} |[\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'}| < \frac{1}{L - 1},
    \end{equation}
    then $\Theta = \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}) \in \mathcal{C}_L$ is strictly diagonally dominant and Symmetric Positive Definite ($\Theta \succ 0$).
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Reduction direction ($\mathcal{C}_L \to \mathcal{C}_\ell$):} Let $\Theta \in \mathcal{C}_L$. If the off-diagonal entries satisfy:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:reduc_bound}
    \sigma_{\max} := \max_{(j,j') \in \mathcal{I}_L} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| < \frac{1}{\ell - 1},
    \end{equation}
    then $\hat{\Theta} = \mathcal{R}_W(\Theta) \in \mathcal{C}_\ell$ is strictly diagonally dominant and Symmetric Positive Definite ($\hat{\Theta} \succ 0$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof of (a).} For any $(j, j') \in \mathcal{I}_L$, Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} gives:
\begin{equation}
|[\Theta]_{j, j'}| \le \sum_{(t,t')} w_{(j,j')}^{(t,t')} |[\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'}| \le \rho_{\max} \sum_{(t,t')} w_{(j,j')}^{(t,t')} = \rho_{\max}.
\end{equation}
For each row $j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$, the off-diagonal absolute sum satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| \le (L - 1) \rho_{\max} < (L - 1) \cdot \frac{1}{L - 1} = 1 = [\Theta]_{j, j}.
\end{equation}
Thus $\Theta$ is strictly diagonally dominant. By the Gershgorin circle theorem \citep[Theorem~6.1.1]{schurmatapp,horn2012matrix}, every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\Theta$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\lambda \ge [\Theta]_{j, j} - \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| \ge 1 - (L - 1)\rho_{\max} > 0.
\end{equation}
Since $\Theta$ is real symmetric and has strictly positive eigenvalues, $\Theta \succ 0$, so $\Theta \in \mathcal{C}_L$.

\textit{Proof of (b).} For each row $t \in \{1, \ldots, \ell\}$, Definition~\ref{def:reduction_op} yields $|[\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'}| \le \sigma_{\max}$. The off-diagonal row sum satisfies $\sum_{t' \neq t} |[\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'}| \le (\ell - 1)\sigma_{\max} < 1 = [\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t}$. By Gershgorin's theorem, all eigenvalues satisfy $\lambda \ge 1 - (\ell - 1)\sigma_{\max} > 0$, hence $\hat{\Theta} \succ 0$ and $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{C}_\ell$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sufficient Nature and Sharpness Asymmetry of Gershgorin Bounds]
\label{rem:sharpness_discussion}
We emphasize that the condition $\rho_{\max} < \frac{1}{L - 1}$ in Theorem~\ref{th:spd_condition} is a \textit{sufficient}, but not necessary, condition for positive definiteness. Its sharpness exhibits a fundamental signed asymmetry.
On the negative boundary, within the class of equicorrelated matrices $\Theta(\rho) = (1-\rho)I_L + \rho \mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top$ with uniform negative parameter $\rho < 0$, the smallest eigenvalue is $\lambda_{\min} = 1 + (L-1)\rho$. This eigenvalue reaches zero and causes loss of positive definiteness at exactly $\rho = -\frac{1}{L-1}$. Because $\rho_{\max} = |\rho| = -\rho$, the Gershgorin lower bound $\lambda_{\min} \ge 1 - (L-1)\rho_{\max} = 1 + (L-1)\rho$ matches the true minimum eigenvalue identically. Thus, the Gershgorin threshold $\rho_{\max} < \frac{1}{L-1}$ is exact and sharp on the negative boundary, with zero conservatism.

In contrast, for uniform positive correlations $\rho > 0$, the smallest eigenvalue is $\lambda_{\min} = 1 - \rho$, which remains positive for all $\rho \in (0, 1)$ regardless of dimension $L$. Here, the Gershgorin lower bound $1 - (L-1)\rho$ drops below zero as soon as $\rho > \frac{1}{L-1}$, making the bound conservative for positive correlations. For general heterogeneous correlation structures, $\Theta$ may remain positive definite well beyond $\rho_{\max} \ge \frac{1}{L-1}$ whenever off-diagonal signs alternate or cancel out. The central utility of Theorem~\ref{th:spd_condition} lies in providing an instantaneous, a priori certificate of positive definiteness verifiable without eigensolvers.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Convexity and Spectrahedral Geometry of the Admissible Domain]
\label{th:admissible_convexity}
Define the admissible latent domain $\mathcal{A}_L \subset \mathcal{S}_1^\ell$ as the set of all reduced correlation matrices whose PLS reconstruction remains strictly positive definite:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:admissible_domain}
\mathcal{A}_L := \left\{ \hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{C}_\ell : \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}) \in \mathcal{C}_L \right\} = \mathcal{C}_\ell \cap \mathcal{T}_{G_*}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_L).
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Open Convex Topology:} The admissible domain $\mathcal{A}_L$ is an open, bounded, convex set in $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$.
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Spectrahedral Closure:} The topological closure $\overline{\mathcal{A}_L}$ is a compact convex spectrahedron, expressible as a joint Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI) in the off-diagonal parameter vector $\hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lmi_admissible}
    \overline{\mathcal{A}_L} = \left\{ \hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}} : \begin{pmatrix} \hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) & \bm{0} \\ \bm{0} & \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}})) \end{pmatrix} \succeq 0 \right\}.
    \end{equation}
    \item[(c)] \textbf{Convex Core:} $\mathcal{A}_L$ contains the Gershgorin open ball $\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{L-1}\right) = \left\{ \hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{C}_\ell : \max_{(t, t')} |[\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'}| < \frac{1}{L-1} \right\}$ as a non-empty convex core.
    \item[(d)] \textbf{Absence of Spurious Feasibility Traps:} The admissible region $\mathcal{A}_L$ is path-connected and contractible to the identity matrix $I_\ell$, guaranteeing that no disconnected feasibility pockets or non-convex indentations exist within the positive definite latent search space.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (a), let $\hat{\Theta}_1, \hat{\Theta}_2 \in \mathcal{A}_L$ and $\alpha \in [0, 1]$. Let $\hat{\Theta}_\alpha = (1-\alpha)\hat{\Theta}_1 + \alpha \hat{\Theta}_2$. Since the correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_\ell$ is convex \citep{Pelamatti}, $\hat{\Theta}_\alpha \in \mathcal{C}_\ell$. By Definition~\ref{def:operator}, the off-diagonal entries of $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})$ are linear combinations of the off-diagonal entries of $\hat{\Theta}$, while diagonal entries are fixed to $1$. Thus $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}$ is an affine operator, satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}_\alpha) = (1 - \alpha) \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}_1) + \alpha \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}_2).
\end{equation}
Since $\hat{\Theta}_1, \hat{\Theta}_2 \in \mathcal{A}_L$, both $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}_1) \succ 0$ and $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}_2) \succ 0$. The cone of symmetric positive definite matrices $\mathbb{S}_{++}^L$ is convex; therefore, any convex combination of positive definite matrices is strictly positive definite. Hence $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}_\alpha) \succ 0$, which proves $\hat{\Theta}_\alpha \in \mathcal{A}_L$.

For (b), let $\mathcal{S} = \left\{ \hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}} : \hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) \succeq 0 \text{ and } \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}})) \succeq 0 \right\}$ denote the solution set of the joint $(\ell + L) \times (\ell + L)$ block-diagonal LMI in \eqnref{eq:lmi_admissible}. Because $\mathcal{A}_L = \left\{ \hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}} : \hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) \succ 0 \text{ and } \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}})) \succ 0 \right\}$, the topological closure $\overline{\mathcal{A}_L}$ is trivially contained in the closed set $\mathcal{S}$. Conversely, let $\hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathcal{S}$. Consider the relative interior point $\bm{0} \in \mathcal{A}_L$ corresponding to $\hat{\Theta}(\bm{0}) = I_\ell$, where $\lambda_{\min}(I_\ell) = 1 > 0$ and $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(I_\ell)) = \lambda_{\min}(I_L) = 1 > 0$. For any $t \in (0, 1]$, define the linear interpolation sequence $\hat{\bm{\theta}}_t = (1-t)\hat{\bm{\theta}}$. By linearity of the parameterization and strict positive definiteness of $I$,
\begin{equation}
\hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_t) = (1-t)\hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) + t I_\ell \succ 0, \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_t)) = (1-t)\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}(\hat{\bm{\theta}})) + t I_L \succ 0.
\end{equation}
Therefore $\hat{\bm{\theta}}_t \in \mathcal{A}_L$ for all $t \in (0, 1]$. Taking the limit as $t \to 0^+$ gives $\lim_{t \to 0^+} \hat{\bm{\theta}}_t = \hat{\bm{\theta}}$, which proves that every point of $\mathcal{S}$ is an accumulation point of $\mathcal{A}_L$, establishing the exact equality $\overline{\mathcal{A}_L} = \mathcal{S}$. Boundedness follows from $\overline{\mathcal{A}_L} \subset \mathcal{H}_\ell = [-1, 1]^{D_{\outp}}$, confirming that $\overline{\mathcal{A}_L}$ is a compact convex spectrahedron.

For (c), Theorem~\ref{th:spd_condition}(a) proves that any $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{C}_\ell$ with $\rho_{\max} < \frac{1}{L-1}$ satisfies $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}) \succ 0$, placing the entire open ball inside $\mathcal{A}_L$. Since $\lambda_{\min}$ is a continuous function of matrix entries, $\mathcal{A}_L$ is open.

For (d), since $\mathcal{A}_L$ is convex and contains $I_\ell$ (where $\rho_{\max} = 0 < \frac{1}{L-1}$), for any $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{A}_L$, the line segment $\gamma(\alpha) = (1-\alpha)\hat{\Theta} + \alpha I_\ell$ lies entirely in $\mathcal{A}_L$ for all $\alpha \in [0, 1]$. This defines a deformation retraction of $\mathcal{A}_L$ onto $\{I_\ell\}$, establishing contractibility and path-connectedness.
\end{proof}


\begin{remark}[Asymmetry of Reduction and Reconstruction]
\label{rem:asymmetry}
Because $\ell \ll L$, we have $\frac{1}{\ell - 1} \gg \frac{1}{L - 1}$. For example, with $L = 20$ levels and $\ell = 3$ latent components:
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{L - 1} \approx 0.053, \quad \text{whereas} \quad \frac{1}{\ell - 1} = 0.500.
\end{equation}
Thus, compressing an $L \times L$ correlation matrix down to $\ell \times \ell$ preserves positive definiteness over an order-of-magnitude broader range of correlation strengths ($9.5\times$ wider) than reconstructing from $\ell \times \ell$ up to $L \times L$ (see Table~\ref{tab:operator_comparison} in Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_operator_duality} for a complete property-by-property comparison).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Fast \texorpdfstring{$O(L^2)$}{O(L^2)} Ledoit--Wolf Spectral Repair Operators}
\label{subsec:repair_operators}

When the latent correlation $\rho_{\max}$ violates the sufficient Gershgorin threshold $\frac{1}{L-1}$, the reconstructed matrix $\Theta = \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})$ is not guaranteed to remain positive definite, and indeed frequently develops negative eigenvalues (as characterized analytically by Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral_bounds} and confirmed numerically in Section~\ref{sec:simulations}). In early engineering implementations \citep{saves2023high,saves2023smt}, numerical non-definiteness was handled by adding an un-normalized diagonal nugget $\Theta_{\mathrm{nugget}} = \Theta + \eta I_L$. While shifting eigenvalues by $+\eta$, this ad-hoc intervention produces severe statistical and geometric pathologies: it violates the unit-diagonal constraint of the correlation elliptope ($[\Theta_{\mathrm{nugget}}]_{j, j} = 1 + \eta \neq 1$) and artificially inflates the Gaussian process variance to $\sigma^2(1+\eta)$, systematically miscalibrating posterior predictive intervals. If entrywise renormalization $[\Theta]_{j, j'} / (1+\eta)$ is subsequently attempted, positive definiteness is generally destroyed.

To overcome these pathologies without incurring the prohibitive $O(L^3)$ computational cost of Higham's alternating projections, we introduce the closed-form diagonal shift-and-scaling repair operator and establish its invariance on the correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_L$ for any user-specified spectral tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$.

\begin{theorem}[Fast $O(L^2)$ Spectral Repair and Unit-Diagonal Invariance]
\label{th:reg_spd}\label{prop:reg_spd}
Let $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{H}_\ell$ (or $\mathcal{C}_\ell$) and let $\Theta = \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}) \in \mathcal{S}_1^L$ with $[\Theta]_{j, j} = 1$ for all $j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$. For any user-specified spectral tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$ (e.g., $10^{-6}$), define the shift parameter $\eta \ge 0$ by either:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact Spectral Shift:}
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:eta_exact}
    \eta_{\mathrm{exact}} = \max\bigl(0,\; -\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \varepsilon\bigr),
    \end{equation}
    where $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta)$ denotes the smallest eigenvalue of $\Theta$;
    \item \textbf{Fast Gershgorin Shift:}
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:eta_gersh}
    \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} = \max\left(0,\; \max_{1 \le j \le L} \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| - 1 + \varepsilon\right); \quad \text{or}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Mean-Deflated Weyl--Gershgorin (MDG) Shift:} Define the non-negative mean off-diagonal correlation:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mean_rho_plus}
    \bar{\rho}_+(\Theta) = \max\left(0,\; \frac{1}{L(L-1)} \sum_{1 \le j \neq j' \le L} [\Theta]_{j, j'}\right) \in [0, 1].
    \end{equation}
    The Mean-Deflated Gershgorin shift is defined in closed form by:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:eta_mdg}
    \eta_{\mathrm{MDG}} = \max\left(0,\; \bar{\rho}_+(\Theta) + \max_{1 \le j \le L} \sum_{j' \neq j} \big|[\Theta]_{j, j'} - \bar{\rho}_+(\Theta)\big| - 1 + \varepsilon\right),
    \end{equation}
    with adaptive point-wise envelope $\eta_{\mathrm{MDG}^*} = \min(\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}, \eta_{\mathrm{MDG}})$.
\end{enumerate}
Then the repaired matrix, expressible as a closed-form linear shrinkage toward the identity target $I_L$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regularization}
\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \frac{\Theta + \eta I_L}{1 + \eta} = (1 - \alpha) \Theta + \alpha I_L, \quad \text{with shrinkage intensity } \alpha = \frac{\eta}{1 + \eta} \in [0, 1),
\end{equation}
satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Guaranteed Positive Definiteness:} $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} \succ 0$ for any $\varepsilon > 0$, with smallest eigenvalue bounded below by $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}) \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{1 + \eta} > 0$;
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Exact Diagonal Preservation:} $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j} = 1$ for all $j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$, so $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} \in \mathcal{C}_L$;
    \item[(c)] \textbf{Identity on Valid Matrices and Shrinkage Behavior:} Under the exact spectral shift $\eta_{\mathrm{exact}}$ \eqnref{eq:eta_exact}, $\eta_{\mathrm{exact}} = 0$ if and only if $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) \ge \varepsilon$, leaving all positive definite matrices satisfying the spectral floor unmodified. Under the fast Gershgorin shift $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$ \eqnref{eq:eta_gersh}, $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} = 0$ if and only if $\Theta$ satisfies strict diagonal dominance with margin $\varepsilon$ ($\max_{1 \le j \le L} \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| \le 1 - \varepsilon$). If $\Theta \succ 0$ is positive definite but not diagonally dominant, Gershgorin repair applies non-zero shrinkage $\alpha = \frac{\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}} > 0$ toward the identity $I_L$, capping the average off-diagonal absolute correlation in any row by $\frac{1-\varepsilon}{L-1}$;
    \item[(d)] \textbf{Elimination of Over-Shrinkage on Positive Correlations:} Under the Mean-Deflated Gershgorin shift $\eta_{\mathrm{MDG}}$ \eqnref{eq:eta_mdg}, for any equicorrelated correlation matrix with parameter $\rho \in [0, 1 - \varepsilon]$, $\bar{\rho}_+(\Theta) = \rho$ and $[\Theta]_{j, j'} - \bar{\rho}_+(\Theta) \equiv 0$, yielding $\eta_{\mathrm{MDG}} = \max(0, \rho - 1 + \varepsilon) = 0$. Consequently, $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \Theta$ remains unmodified across the positive equicorrelation spectrum $\rho \in [0, 1 - \varepsilon]$, eliminating the $\frac{1-\varepsilon}{L-1}$ over-shrinkage cap of naive Gershgorin repair while executing in $O(L^2)$ flops without eigensolvers;
    \item[(e)] \textbf{Eigenvector Invariance and Commutation:} The repair operator preserves the exact eigenvectors of $\Theta$. Specifically, $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}, \Theta] = \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} \Theta - \Theta \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \bm{0}$, and every eigenvector $v_i$ of $\Theta$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_i(\Theta)$ is an eigenvector of $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}$ with repaired eigenvalue $\lambda_i(\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}) = \frac{\lambda_i(\Theta) + \eta}{1 + \eta}$;
    \item[(f)] \textbf{Exact Off-Diagonal Ratio Preservation and Minimal Ray Shrinkage:} For any two pairs of distinct off-diagonal indices $(j_1, j_1')$ and $(j_2, j_2')$, the relative correlation ratio is strictly preserved:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ratio_preservation}
    \frac{[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j_1, j_1'}}{[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j_2, j_2'}} = \frac{[\Theta]_{j_1, j_1'}}{[\Theta]_{j_2, j_2'}}.
    \end{equation}
    Furthermore, for any indefinite matrix with $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) < 0$, the exact spectral shift $\eta_{\mathrm{exact}} = -\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \varepsilon$ yields the minimal linear shrinkage intensity $\alpha^* = \frac{\eta_{\mathrm{exact}}}{1+\eta_{\mathrm{exact}}}$ along the ray $\Theta(\alpha) = (1 - \alpha)\Theta + \alpha I_L$ required to satisfy $\lambda_{\min}\bigl(\Theta(\alpha)\bigr) \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{1+\eta_{\mathrm{exact}}}$, minimizing the distortion $\|\Theta(\alpha) - \Theta\|_p = \alpha \|\Theta - I_L\|_p$ across all unitarily invariant Schatten $p$-norms among valid restorations along this ray. For conservative shifts ($\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{MDG}}$), the repair trades minimal metric distortion for closed-form $O(L^2)$ evaluation and analytical gradient availability.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues of $\Theta + \eta I_L$ are $\lambda_i(\Theta) + \eta$. Under the exact spectral shift $\eta_{\mathrm{exact}}$, if $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) < \varepsilon$, then $\eta = -\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \varepsilon > 0$, ensuring $\lambda_i + \eta \ge \lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + (-\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \varepsilon) = \varepsilon > 0$. Under the fast Gershgorin shift $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$, Gershgorin's circle theorem guarantees that $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) \ge 1 - \max_j \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}|$. Hence $-\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) \le \max_j \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| - 1$, implying $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} \ge -\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \varepsilon$, which also ensures $\lambda_i + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} \ge \varepsilon > 0$.

Under the Mean-Deflated Gershgorin shift $\eta_{\mathrm{MDG}}$, decompose $\Theta = \Theta_{\mathrm{eq}}(\bar{\rho}_+) + \Delta$, where $\Theta_{\mathrm{eq}}(\bar{\rho}_+) = (1 - \bar{\rho}_+) I_L + \bar{\rho}_+ \mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top$ and $\Delta = \Theta - \Theta_{\mathrm{eq}}(\bar{\rho}_+)$ has zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries $[\Delta]_{j, j'} = [\Theta]_{j, j'} - \bar{\rho}_+$. Because $\bar{\rho}_+ \ge 0$, the smallest eigenvalue of $\Theta_{\mathrm{eq}}(\bar{\rho}_+)$ is $1 - \bar{\rho}_+$. By Weyl's eigenvalue inequality and Gershgorin's circle theorem applied to $\Delta$, $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) \ge \lambda_{\min}\bigl(\Theta_{\mathrm{eq}}(\bar{\rho}_+)\bigr) + \lambda_{\min}(\Delta) \ge (1 - \bar{\rho}_+) - \max_{1 \le j \le L} \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'} - \bar{\rho}_+|$, which guarantees $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \eta_{\mathrm{MDG}} \ge \varepsilon > 0$.

Dividing by $1 + \eta \ge 1 > 0$ preserves strict positivity: $\lambda_i(\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}) \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{1 + \eta} > 0$. The diagonal entries satisfy $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j} = \frac{1 + \eta \cdot 1}{1 + \eta} = 1$ for all $j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$. When $\Theta$ satisfies $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) \ge \varepsilon$, $\eta_{\mathrm{exact}} = 0$ and $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \Theta$ remains strictly unmodified. Under Gershgorin repair, $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} = 0$ if and only if $\max_j \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| \le 1 - \varepsilon$. When $\Theta \succ 0$ fails strict diagonal dominance, $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} > 0$, and the repaired matrix $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \frac{\Theta + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} I_L}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}$ satisfies $\sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'}| \le \frac{R_{\max}}{1 + R_{\max} - 1 + \varepsilon} = \frac{R_{\max}}{R_{\max} + \varepsilon} < 1$, strictly enforcing diagonal dominance. For (d), substituting $[\Theta]_{j, j'} = \rho \ge 0$ gives $\bar{\rho}_+ = \rho$ and $[\Theta]_{j, j'} - \bar{\rho}_+ \equiv 0$, collapsing the sum to zero and giving $\eta_{\mathrm{MDG}} = 0$ for all $\rho \in [0, 1 - \varepsilon]$.

For (e), because $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \frac{1}{1+\eta}\Theta + \frac{\eta}{1+\eta}I_L$, we compute $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} \Theta = \frac{1}{1+\eta}\Theta^2 + \frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\Theta = \Theta \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}$, so $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}, \Theta] = \bm{0}$. For any eigenvector $v_i$ with $\Theta v_i = \lambda_i v_i$, $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} v_i = \frac{\lambda_i + \eta}{1+\eta} v_i$, preserving all eigenvectors identically.
For (f), for any $j \neq j'$, $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'} = \frac{1}{1+\eta}[\Theta]_{j, j'}$, so the factor $\frac{1}{1+\eta}$ cancels identically in the ratio between any two off-diagonal entries. Along the linear shrinkage ray $\Theta(\alpha) = (1-\alpha)\Theta + \alpha I_L$, the distortion $\|\Theta(\alpha) - \Theta\|_p = \alpha \|\Theta - I_L\|_p$ is strictly increasing in $\alpha \ge 0$. For an indefinite matrix ($\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) < 0$), the smallest shrinkage parameter $\alpha \ge 0$ satisfying $\lambda_{\min}\bigl(\Theta(\alpha)\bigr) = (1-\alpha)\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \alpha \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{1+\eta_{\mathrm{exact}}}$ is uniquely achieved at $\alpha^* = \frac{-\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \varepsilon}{1 - \lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \varepsilon} = \frac{\eta_{\mathrm{exact}}}{1+\eta_{\mathrm{exact}}}$, minimizing all unitarily invariant Schatten $p$-norms along this ray.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Closed-Form Heteroscedastic Covariance Spectral Repair]
\label{cor:heteroscedastic_repair}
Let $\Sigma = D \Theta D \in \mathcal{S}^L$ be a heteroscedastic qualitative covariance matrix, where $D = \diag(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_L) \succ 0$ with category-dependent standard deviations $\sigma_j > 0$ and $[\Sigma]_{j, j} = \sigma_j^2$. If the underlying correlation matrix $\Theta = D^{-1} \Sigma D^{-1} \in \mathcal{S}_1^L$ develops negative eigenvalues, then for any spectral tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$ and corresponding shift $\eta \ge 0$ (exact, Gershgorin, or MDG from Theorem~\ref{th:reg_spd}), the repaired covariance matrix defined by:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:heteroscedastic_repair}
\Sigma_{\mathrm{SPD}} := \frac{\Sigma + \eta D^2}{1 + \eta} = (1 - \alpha) \Sigma + \alpha D^2, \quad \text{with } \alpha = \frac{\eta}{1 + \eta},
\end{equation}
satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
    \item \textbf{Exact Marginal Variance Preservation:} $[\Sigma_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j} = \sigma_j^2$ for all $j \in \{1, \dots, L\}$.
    \item \textbf{Guaranteed Positive Definiteness:} $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SPD}} = D \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} D \succ 0$, with smallest eigenvalue bounded below by:
    \begin{equation}
    \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{SPD}}) \ge \left( \min_{1 \le j \le L} \sigma_j^2 \right) \frac{\varepsilon}{1 + \eta} > 0.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Fast $O(L^2)$ Evaluation:} Computing $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SPD}}$ requires zero eigensolvers and executes in $O(L^2)$ flops.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Factoring out $D$ yields $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \frac{D \Theta D + \eta D I_L D}{1+\eta} = D \left( \frac{\Theta + \eta I_L}{1+\eta} \right) D = D \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} D$.
For diagonal entries, $[\Sigma_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j} = \frac{\sigma_j^2 + \eta \sigma_j^2}{1+\eta} = \sigma_j^2$.
By Ostrowski's congruence theorem (or the Courant--Fischer min-max theorem), since $D \succ 0$ is diagonal and non-singular, $\lambda_{\min}(D \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} D) \ge \sigma_{\min}(D)^2 \lambda_{\min}(\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}) = (\min_{1 \le j \le L} \sigma_j^2) \lambda_{\min}(\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}) \ge (\min_{1 \le j \le L} \sigma_j^2) \frac{\varepsilon}{1+\eta} > 0$, guaranteeing positive definiteness.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Statistical Shrinkage Interpretation and Computational Complexity]
\label{rem:repair_complexity}
The repair transformation $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = (1-\alpha)\Theta + \alpha I_L$ belongs to the family of linear shrinkage estimators toward the well-conditioned identity target $I_L$ \citep{ledoit2004well,schafer2005shrinkage}. In classical sample covariance estimation, the shrinkage intensity $\alpha$ is chosen asymptotically to minimize Mean Squared Error under Gaussian assumptions from sample replicates. In our framework, $\Theta$ is a parametric correlation matrix evaluated at candidate hyperparameters rather than an empirical sample covariance. Gershgorin circle theory provides an exact, closed-form shrinkage intensity $\alpha_G = \frac{\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}$ guaranteeing the geometric constraint $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} \in \mathcal{C}_L$ in $O(L^2)$ flops, bypassing iterative $O(k_{\mathrm{iter}} L^3)$ alternating Dykstra projections \citep{higham2002computing}.

Furthermore, whereas computing full eigenspectra costs $O(L^3)$ flops, the exact spectral shift $\eta_{\mathrm{exact}}$ can be computed in $O(k L^2)$ flops via shifted Lanczos iterations on $B(\Theta) = U(\Theta)I_L - \Theta$. When $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta)$ is a simple eigenvalue with normalized eigenvector $u_*$, the Hellmann--Feynman theorem yields the analytical gradient:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hellmann_feynman}
\frac{\partial \eta_{\mathrm{exact}}}{\partial \theta_p} = -u_*^\top \frac{\partial \Theta}{\partial \theta_p} u_* = -2 \sum_{1 \le j < j' \le L} [u_*]_j [u_*]_{j'} \left([G_*]_{\psi(j,j',L), p}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
evaluating in $O(D_{\outp} L^2) = O(\ell^2 L^2)$ flops. Finally, unlike the raw nugget $\Theta + \eta I_L$ which violates unit diagonals ($[\Theta]_{j, j} = 1 + \eta$) and distorts process variance, the shrinkage operator \eqnref{eq:regularization} rescales all entries symmetrically by $(1+\eta)^{-1}$, maintaining unit diagonals and ensuring positive definiteness. This multi-criteria trade-off between metric distortion and conditioning across all repair strategies, along with the exact elimination of over-shrinkage by MDG on positive correlation spectra, is illustrated empirically in Figure~\ref{fig:phase_diagram}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Analytical Jacobian and Directional Gradients of Gershgorin Repair]
\label{th:gershgorin_gradient}
Let $\hat{\bm{\theta}} = (\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_{D_{\outp}})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$ denote the free off-diagonal latent correlation parameters, where $\theta_p = [\hat{\Theta}]_{\psi^{-1}(p, \ell)}$. Let $\Theta = \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})$ and let $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}$ be the repaired matrix under the fast Gershgorin shift $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$ \eqnref{eq:eta_gersh}.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Inactive Regularization Regime ($\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} = 0$):} On the open set where $\Theta$ satisfies strict diagonal dominance ($\max_j \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| < 1 - \varepsilon$), $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \Theta$ is smooth with linear Jacobian:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:grad_inactive}
    \frac{\partial [\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'}}{\partial \theta_p} =
    \begin{cases}
    0, & \text{if } j = j', \\
    \left( [G_*]_{\psi(j,j',L), p} \right)^2, & \text{if } j \neq j'.
    \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Active Regularization Regime ($\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} > 0$):} Let $j^* = \arg\max_{1 \le j \le L} \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}|$. On any open polyhedral chamber where $j^*$ is unique and $[\Theta]_{j^*, j'} \neq 0$ for all $j' \neq j^*$, the shift derivative with respect to $\theta_p$ is:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:eta_gradient}
    \frac{\partial \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}{\partial \theta_p} = \sum_{j' \neq j^*} \operatorname{sgn}\bigl([\Theta]_{j^*, j'}\bigr) \left( [G_*]_{\psi(\min(j^*, j'), \max(j^*, j'), L), p} \right)^2.
    \end{equation}
    Consequently, the exact closed-form partial derivative of the repaired matrix entry $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'}$ is:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:repaired_entry_gradient}
    \frac{\partial [\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'}}{\partial \theta_p} =
    \begin{cases}
    0, & \text{if } j = j', \\
    \displaystyle\frac{1}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}} \left( \left([G_*]_{\psi(j,j',L), p}\right)^2 - [\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'} \frac{\partial \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}{\partial \theta_p} \right), & \text{if } j \neq j'.
    \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
    \item[(c)] \textbf{Algorithmic Complexity:} Evaluating the complete gradient tensor $\nabla_{\hat{\bm{\theta}}} \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L \times D_{\outp}}$ executes in $O(D_{\outp} L) = O(\ell^2 L)$ flops for the active row $j^*$, and $O(D_{\outp} L^2) = O(\ell^2 L^2)$ flops for all entries, with zero eigenvalue factorizations.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (a), when $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} = 0$ locally, $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \Theta$ remains unmodified. By Definition~\ref{def:operator}, $[\Theta]_{j, j} = 1$ is constant (so its derivative vanishes), and for $j \neq j'$, $[\Theta]_{j, j'} = \sum_{q=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*]_{\psi(j, j', L), q})^2 \theta_q$, yielding \eqnref{eq:grad_inactive}.

For (b), when $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} > 0$ on an open polyhedral chamber where $j^*$ is unique and all off-diagonal entries $[\Theta]_{j^*, j'} \neq 0$, the absolute value function $|u|$ is differentiable with $\frac{\mathrm{d}|u|}{\mathrm{d}u} = \operatorname{sgn}(u)$. Differentiating $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} = \sum_{j' \neq j^*} |[\Theta]_{j^*, j'}| - 1 + \varepsilon$ with respect to $\theta_p$ yields \eqnref{eq:eta_gradient}. For diagonal entries, $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j} = 1$ for all $\hat{\bm{\theta}}$, so its derivative is identically zero. For $j \neq j'$, applying the quotient rule to $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'} = \frac{[\Theta]_{j, j'}}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}$ gives:
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial [\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'}}{\partial \theta_p} &= \frac{\frac{\partial [\Theta]_{j, j'}}{\partial \theta_p} (1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}) - [\Theta]_{j, j'} \frac{\partial \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}{\partial \theta_p}}{(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}})^2} \nonumber \\
&= \frac{1}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}} \left( \frac{\partial [\Theta]_{j, j'}}{\partial \theta_p} - \frac{[\Theta]_{j, j'}}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}} \frac{\partial \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}{\partial \theta_p} \right).
\end{align}
Substituting $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'} = \frac{[\Theta]_{j, j'}}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}$ and $\frac{\partial [\Theta]_{j, j'}}{\partial \theta_p} = ([G_*]_{\psi(j, j', L), p})^2$ establishes \eqnref{eq:repaired_entry_gradient}.

For (c), computing $\frac{\partial \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}{\partial \theta_p}$ requires summing over $L-1$ terms, which takes $O(L)$ flops per parameter $p$, totaling $O(D_{\outp} L)$ flops. Updating all $L(L-1)/2$ off-diagonal entries then requires $O(1)$ flops per entry, resulting in $O(D_{\outp} L^2) = O(\ell^2 L^2)$ flops for the entire gradient tensor without any eigensolvers.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Chain-Rule Jacobian under Latent Hypersphere Parameterization]
\label{cor:hypersphere_chain_rule}
Let the latent correlation matrix $\hat{\Theta}(\Phi) = \hat{L}(\Phi)\hat{L}(\Phi)^\top \in \mathcal{C}_\ell$ be parameterized via its lower-triangular Cholesky factor $\hat{L}(\Phi) \in \mathbb{R}^{\ell \times \ell}$ in spherical angles $\Phi = \{\phi_{t,s}\}_{1 \le s < t \le \ell} \in (0, \pi)^{D_{\outp}}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cholesky_angles}
[\hat{L}(\Phi)]_{t,s} =
\begin{cases}
\cos(\phi_{t,1}), & \text{if } s = 1, \\
\cos(\phi_{t,s}) \prod_{m=1}^{s-1} \sin(\phi_{t,m}), & \text{if } 2 \le s < t, \\
\prod_{m=1}^{t-1} \sin(\phi_{t,m}), & \text{if } s = t \ge 2,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $[\hat{L}]_{1,1} = 1$. For any latent off-diagonal pair $p = \psi(t, t', \ell)$ ($1 \le t < t' \le \ell$), $\theta_p(\Phi) = [\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'} = \sum_{s=1}^t [\hat{L}(\Phi)]_{t,s}[\hat{L}(\Phi)]_{t',s}$. By the matrix chain rule, the gradient of the repaired full correlation matrix $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}$ with respect to each spherical angle $\phi_{a,b}$ ($1 \le b < a \le \ell$) is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hypersphere_chain_rule}
\frac{\partial [\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'}}{\partial \phi_{a,b}} = \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} \frac{\partial [\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'}}{\partial \theta_p} \frac{\partial \theta_p}{\partial \phi_{a,b}},
\end{equation}
where $\frac{\partial [\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j'}}{\partial \theta_p}$ is given in closed form by Theorem~\ref{th:gershgorin_gradient}, and $\frac{\partial \theta_p}{\partial \phi_{a,b}}$ is non-zero only if $a \in \{t, t'\}$, introducing $O(\ell^3)$ overhead per likelihood evaluation (which is $O(1)$ with respect to full dimension $L$).
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Piecewise Smoothness and Clarke Subdifferential Geometry.}
The fast Gershgorin shift $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}(\hat{\Theta})$ is a continuous, piecewise-smooth function composed of absolute values and pointwise maxima. From a non-smooth analysis perspective \citep{clarke1990optimization}, the parameter space $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$ is partitioned into a finite polyhedral complex $\{\mathcal{P}_k\}_{k=1}^K$ on whose interiors $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$ is an exact affine function. Non-differentiability occurs exclusively on the transition boundaries $\partial \mathcal{P}_k$ (where the maximal row index $j^*$ changes or off-diagonal entries cross zero), which form a closed set of Lebesgue measure zero. Consequently, the profile log-likelihood is locally $C^\infty$ on open polyhedral chambers, enabling quasi-Newton routines such as L-BFGS-B to execute stable line searches without gradient distortion. We refer to Proposition~\ref{prop:clarke_subdiff} in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_nonsmooth} for the formal Clarke generalized gradient characterization.

\subsection{Analytical Mechanism of the Nugget and Spectral Extremes}
\label{subsec:nugget_spectrum}

\begin{proposition}[Equicorrelation Spectrum, Determinant, and Spectral Bounds]
\label{th:equicorr}\label{prop:equicorr_spectrum}\label{prop:spectral_bounds}\label{th:spectral_bounds}\label{cor:det}
Let Assumption~\ref{ass:unit_row_norm} hold, and let $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{C}_\ell$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Equicorrelation Preservation and Admissibility:} If $[\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'} = \rho$ for all $(t, t') \in \mathcal{I}_\ell$ with $\rho \in (-1/(\ell-1), 1)$, then $\Theta = \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})$ is equicorrelated with identical parameter $[\Theta]_{j, j'} = \rho$ for all $(j, j') \in \mathcal{I}_L$. Its spectrum consists of $\lambda_1 = 1 + (L-1)\rho$ (multiplicity $1$) and $\lambda_2 = \cdots = \lambda_L = 1 - \rho$ (multiplicity $L-1$). Consequently, $\Theta \succ 0$ if and only if
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:equicorr_interval}
    \rho \in \left(-\frac{1}{L - 1},\; 1\right),
    \end{equation}
    and its determinant is given by
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:det_equicorr}
    \det(\Theta) = \bigl(1 + (L-1)\rho\bigr) (1 - \rho)^{L-1}.
    \end{equation}
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Spectral Bounds and Condition Number:} For any $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{C}_\ell$ with $\rho_{\max} = \max_{(t,t') \in \mathcal{I}_\ell} |[\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'}|$, every eigenvalue $\lambda_i(\Theta)$ satisfies
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:spectral_bounds}
    1 - (L - 1)\rho_{\max} \le \lambda_i(\Theta) \le 1 + (L - 1)\rho_{\max}, \quad \forall i \in \{1, \ldots, L\},
    \end{equation}
    with $\Tr(\Theta) = L$ and mean eigenvalue identically equal to $1$. When $\rho_{\max} < 1/(L-1)$, the spectral condition number satisfies
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cond_bound}
    \kappa(\Theta) \le \frac{1 + (L - 1)\rho_{\max}}{1 - (L - 1)\rho_{\max}}.
    \end{equation}
    The upper bound in~\eqnref{eq:spectral_bounds} is sharp and attained when $\hat{\Theta}$ is equicorrelated with parameter $\rho = \rho_{\max} > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For (a), by Lemma~\ref{lem:convex}, each off-diagonal entry $[\Theta]_{j, j'}$ is a convex combination of the off-diagonal entries of $\hat{\Theta}$, hence $[\Theta]_{j, j'} = \rho \sum_{(t,t')} w_{(j,j')}^{(t,t')} = \rho$. The matrix takes the compound symmetry form $\Theta = (1 - \rho) I_L + \rho \mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top$. The standard eigenspaces are $\operatorname{span}\{\mathds{1}_L\}$ with eigenvalue $1 + (L-1)\rho$ and $\mathds{1}_L^\perp$ with eigenvalue $1 - \rho$, from which \eqnref{eq:equicorr_interval} and the determinant product \eqnref{eq:det_equicorr} follow directly.

For (b), by Lemma~\ref{lem:convex}, $|[\Theta]_{j, j'}| \le \rho_{\max}$, so the Gershgorin radius for each row satisfies $R_j = \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| \le (L-1)\rho_{\max}$. The Gershgorin circle theorem places all eigenvalues within $\bigcup_{j=1}^L [1 - R_j, 1 + R_j] \subseteq [1 - (L-1)\rho_{\max}, 1 + (L-1)\rho_{\max}]$. Since $[\Theta]_{j, j} = 1$, $\Tr(\Theta) = L$. When $\rho_{\max} < 1/(L-1)$, $1 - (L-1)\rho_{\max} > 0$, yielding~\eqnref{eq:cond_bound}. Sharpness of the upper envelope follows by setting $\rho = \rho_{\max} > 0$ in part (a).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Structural Mechanism of the Nugget and Gershgorin Boundary]
\label{rem:nugget}
Proposition~\ref{th:equicorr} exposes the structural mechanism governing the empirical nugget requirement noted in \citet{saves2023high}. When level correlations are non-negative ($\rho \ge 0$), the spectrum $\lambda_1 = 1 + (L-1)\rho \ge 1$ and $\lambda_2 = \cdots = \lambda_L = 1 - \rho > 0$ remains strictly positive for all $L \ge 2$, exhibiting no dimensional boundary contraction. This explains why low-dimensional engineering benchmarks with predominantly positive qualitative affinities \citep{saves2023high} rarely encountered indefinite reconstructions. Conversely, when correlations are negative ($\rho < 0$), the minimum eigenvalue is $\lambda_{\min} = 1 + (L-1)\rho$. Because $\ell < L$, the admissible lower bound contracts from $-1/(\ell-1)$ to $-1/(L-1)$. For any $\rho \in (-1/(\ell-1), -1/(L-1)]$, the latent matrix $\hat{\Theta}$ is valid while $\Theta$ develops a non-positive eigenvalue $\lambda_1 \le 0$. Along this negative boundary, the Gershgorin condition of Theorem~\ref{th:spd_condition} ($\rho_{\max} < 1/(L-1)$) is sharp. While conservative on the positive cone $\rho \in [0, 1)$, the Gershgorin bound provides a tight certificate against indefinite configurations in the contracted boundary zone.
\end{remark}

\section{Sample Gram Matrices and Categorical Correlation Kernels}
\label{sec:kernels}

\subsection{Sample Gram Matrix Positive Semidefiniteness and PLS Coordinate Recovery}
\label{subsec:gram_matrix_recovery}

In Gaussian process emulation with qualitative inputs, the covariance matrix of the observed data vector depends directly on the sample Gram matrix induced by the categorical correlation function. Furthermore, computational implementations of qualitative kriging (such as the Surrogate Modeling Toolbox, SMT \citep{saves2023smt}) frequently evaluate cross-level correlations on-the-fly via inner products of indicator vectors rather than storing dense $L \times L$ correlation matrices in memory. In this subsection, we establish the positive semidefiniteness and spectral scaling of the sample Gram matrix, and prove the exact mathematical equivalence of PLS coordinate recovery.

\begin{proposition}[Sample Gram Matrix Factorization and Spectral Scaling]
\label{prop:gram_psd}\label{th:gram_psd}\label{prop:gram_scaling}
Let $\Theta \in \mathcal{C}_L$. For any sample of $n_t$ categorical observations $(c_1, \ldots, c_{n_t}) \in \{1, \ldots, L\}^{n_t}$, define the sample Gram matrix $K \in \mathbb{R}^{n_t \times n_t}$ by $[K]_{r, s} = [\Theta]_{c_r, c_s}$ for all $r, s \in \{1, \ldots, n_t\}$. Let $M \in \{0, 1\}^{n_t \times L}$ denote the level indicator matrix $[M]_{r, j} = \delta_{c_r, j}$, and let $D_n = M^\top M = \diag(n_1, \ldots, n_L)$, where $n_j = \sum_{r=1}^{n_t} \delta_{c_r, j}$ is the sample count of category $j$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Positive Semidefiniteness:} $K = M \Theta M^\top$ is Symmetric Positive Semidefinite ($K \succeq 0$). Moreover, $K \succ 0$ if and only if $n_t \le L$ and all observations have distinct levels.
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Spectral Identification:} The non-zero eigenvalues of $K$ coincide with the non-zero eigenvalues of $B = D_n \Theta$, all of which are real and strictly positive.
    \item[(c)] \textbf{Balanced Design Scaling:} For balanced designs ($n_j = n_t / L$ for all $j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$, requiring $n_t \ge L$), the spectrum of $K$ consists of $n_t - L$ zeros and $L$ strictly positive eigenvalues that are scaled copies of the spectrum of $\Theta$:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:gram_scaled_eigs}
    \lambda_i(K) = \frac{n_t}{L} \lambda_i(\Theta), \quad \forall i \in \{1, \ldots, L\}.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For (a), symmetry is immediate. For any vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^{n_t}$, the quadratic form satisfies $v^\top K v = (M^\top v)^\top \Theta (M^\top v) \ge 0$ since $\Theta \succ 0$, with equality if and only if $M^\top v = \bm{0}_L$, proving $K \succeq 0$ and $\ker(K) = \ker(M^\top)$. For (b) and (c), Sylvester's cyclic determinant theorem and balanced design scaling follow directly from the similarity of $B = D_n \Theta$ to the positive semidefinite matrix $D_n^{1/2} \Theta D_n^{1/2}$. Complete algebraic derivations are provided in Appendix~\ref{subsec:proof_gram}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Canonical PLS Coordinate Recovery.}
In the canonical qualitative kriging framework \eqnref{eq:qual_kernel}, the correlation between levels $c, c' \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ is evaluated directly via $R_{\mathrm{qual}}(c, c') = [\Theta]_{c, c'}$. Under this formulation, Proposition~\ref{prop:gram_psd} guarantees that $K = M \Theta M^\top \succeq 0$ whenever $\Theta \in \mathcal{C}_L$.

In computational implementations of qualitative kriging (such as the Surrogate Modeling Toolbox, SMT \citep{saves2023smt}), emulators frequently evaluate cross-level correlations on-the-fly via inner products of indicator vectors rather than constructing dense $L \times L$ correlation matrices in memory. To establish the exact mathematical equivalence between this continuous software implementation and our matrix-theoretic framework, we define the pairwise level selection operator $\delta : \{1, \ldots, L\}^2 \to \{0, 1\}^{D_{\inp}}$ by:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta_selection}
\delta(c, c') =
\begin{cases}
\bm{0}_{D_{\inp}}, & \text{if } c = c', \\
\zeta_c \odot \zeta_{c'}, & \text{if } c \neq c',
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\zeta_c$ is the cross-level indicator vector (Definition~\ref{def:cross_level}).

\begin{lemma}[Canonical PLS Coordinate Recovery]
\label{lem:canonical_pls_recov}\label{th:canonical_pls_recov}
Let $\hat{\Theta} \in \mathcal{S}_1^\ell$ and let $G_* \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\inp} \times D_{\outp}}$ satisfy Assumption~\ref{ass:unit_row_norm}. The canonical categorical correlation $[\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})]_{c, c'}$ is recovered exactly in PLS coordinates by:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:canonical_pls_eval}
R_{\mathrm{qual}}(c, c') = \delta_{c, c'} + \sum_{m=1}^{D_{\inp}} \left( \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} \left([G_*]_{m, p}\right)^2 [\hat{\Theta}]_{\psi^{-1}(p, \ell)} \right) [\delta(c, c')]_m,
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{c, c'}$ is the Kronecker delta.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
When $c = c'$, $\delta_{c, c'} = 1$ and $\delta(c, c) = \bm{0}_{D_{\inp}}$, so the summation vanishes, yielding $R_{\mathrm{qual}}(c, c) = 1 = [\Theta]_{c, c}$. When $c \neq c'$, $\delta_{c, c'} = 0$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:hadamard}, $\delta(c, c') = \zeta_c \odot \zeta_{c'} = e_{m^*}$ with $m^* = \psi(\min(c, c'), \max(c, c'), L)$. The summation collapses to $m^*$, which by Definition~\ref{def:operator} equals $[\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})]_{c, c'}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Admissible Distance-Based Kernels on the Correlation Elliptope}
\label{subsec:cat_kernels}\label{subsec:admissible_kernels}

While canonical qualitative kriging parameterizes correlations directly through matrix entries $R_{\mathrm{qual}}(c, c') = [\Theta]_{c, c'}$, an alternative modeling tradition in spatial statistics constructs stationary covariance kernels based on geometric distance metrics \citep{williams2006gaussian,Bouhlel18,Roustant}. In this subsection, we analyze the validity of distance-based categorical kernels under PLS dimension reduction.

\paragraph{The High-Dimensional EHH Motivation and Failure of Naive Exponentiation.}

In its initial conception \citep{saves2023high,Mixed_Paul}, the development of categorical PLS was motivated by the objective of adapting the Exponential Homoscedastic Hypersphere (EHH) model to high-dimensional categorical problems. In continuous KPLS \citep{bouhlel_KPLSK,Bouhlel18}, dimension reduction operates by projecting inputs onto score directions and evaluating an exponential covariance kernel. Transposing this mechanism to qualitative inputs, the preliminary EHH heuristic parameterized categorical covariances via direct entrywise exponentiation $k(c, c') = \exp(-[\Theta]_{c, c'})$ to preserve an exponential structure and maintain positive entries ($k(c, c') > 0$) across all level pairs.

However, as we now demonstrate, this naive entrywise exponentiation does \textit{not} define a valid positive semidefinite kernel in general: correlation entries $[\Theta]_{c, c'}$ are inner products of level vectors rather than metric distances, and can naturally take negative values in $[-1, 1]$.

\begin{example}[Non-PSD Counterexample for Naive Exponentiation]
\label{ex:non_psd_counterexample}
Consider $L = 2$ categorical levels with the valid correlation matrix:
\begin{equation}
\Theta = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The matrix $\Theta$ is positive definite, with eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = 1.5$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.5$. However, evaluating heuristic exponential kernels directly on the correlation entries produces indefinite matrices under both standard conventions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Direct Unit-Diagonal Assignment ($K_{\mathrm{naive}}$):} Setting diagonal entries to $k(c, c) = 1$ and off-diagonal entries to $k(c, c') = \exp(-[\Theta]_{c, c'}) = \exp(0.5) = e^{0.5}$ yields:
    \begin{equation}
    K_{\mathrm{naive}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & e^{0.5} \\ e^{0.5} & 1 \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1.6487 \\ 1.6487 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.
    \end{equation}
    The eigenvalues of $K_{\mathrm{naive}}$ are $\lambda_1 = 1 + e^{0.5} \approx 2.6487$ and $\lambda_2 = 1 - e^{0.5} \approx -0.6487 < 0$, with determinant $\det(K_{\mathrm{naive}}) = 1 - e \approx -1.7183 < 0$.
    \item \textbf{Un-Normalized Exponentiation with Standardized Correlation ($K_{\mathrm{std}}$):} Evaluating un-normalized entrywise exponentiation $[K_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{c, c'} = \exp(-[\Theta]_{c, c'})$ produces diagonal entries $[K_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{c, c} = \exp(-1) = e^{-1} \approx 0.3679$ and off-diagonal entries $[K_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{1, 2} = \exp(0.5) = e^{0.5} \approx 1.6487$. (Directly, $K_{\mathrm{raw}}$ has eigenvalues $e^{-1} \pm e^{0.5} \approx 2.0166$ and $-1.2808 < 0$.) Re-normalizing $K_{\mathrm{raw}}$ to unit diagonals via $D^{-1/2} K_{\mathrm{raw}} D^{-1/2}$ where $D = \diag(K_{\mathrm{raw}})$ yields off-diagonal correlation:
    \begin{equation}
    \frac{[K_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{1, 2}}{\sqrt{[K_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{1, 1} [K_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{2, 2}}} = \frac{e^{0.5}}{\sqrt{e^{-1} \cdot e^{-1}}} = \frac{e^{0.5}}{e^{-1}} = e^{1.5} \approx 4.4817.
    \end{equation}
    The resulting standardized correlation matrix is:
    \begin{equation}
    K_{\mathrm{std}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & e^{1.5} \\ e^{1.5} & 1 \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4.4817 \\ 4.4817 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
    \end{equation}
    whose eigenvalues are $\lambda_1 = 1 + e^{1.5} \approx 5.4817$ and $\lambda_2 = 1 - e^{1.5} \approx -3.4817 < 0$, with determinant $\det(K_{\mathrm{std}}) = 1 - e^3 \approx -19.0855 < 0$.
\end{enumerate}
In both cases, naive exponentiation yields negative eigenvalues, violating positive definiteness.
\end{example}

\paragraph{Metric Geometry and Schoenberg's Characterization.}
The geometric reason for this failure stems from Schoenberg's classical characterization of radial positive definite kernels.

\begin{schoenbergthm}[{\cite{schoenberg1938metric}}]
\label{th:schoenberg}
Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a set and let $d : \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ be a symmetric kernel with $d(x, x) = 0$. The Gaussian/exponential kernel $k(x, x') = \exp(-\alpha d^2(x, x'))$ is Positive Semidefinite for all $\alpha > 0$ if and only if $d^2(x, x')$ is \textbf{Conditionally Negative Definite (CND)}, meaning that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, all $x_1, \ldots, x_n \in \mathcal{X}$, and all $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfying $\sum_{i=1}^n v_i = 0$:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n v_i v_j \, d^2(x_i, x_j) \le 0.
\end{equation}
\end{schoenbergthm}

In Example~\ref{ex:non_psd_counterexample}, the quantity $-[\Theta]_{c, c'}$ is not conditionally negative definite. Furthermore, for any negative correlation $[\Theta]_{c, c'} < 0$, naive exponentiation produces off-diagonal entries $e^{-[\Theta]_{c, c'}} > 1$, which directly violates the fundamental Cauchy--Schwarz reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) inequality:
\begin{equation}
|k(c, c')|^2 \le k(c, c) k(c', c') = 1 \cdot 1 = 1.
\end{equation}
Whenever an off-diagonal correlation entry exceeds $1$, the $2 \times 2$ principal submatrix has determinant $1 - (k(c, c'))^2 < 0$, producing at least one negative eigenvalue, which invalidates the resulting covariance matrix for Gaussian process modeling.

\begin{remark}[Admissibility: Entrywise Positivity vs. Schoenberg Equivalence]
\label{rem:schoenberg_sharpness}
The failure of naive exponentiation illustrates a basic matrix-theoretic distinction: an entrywise positive symmetric matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L}$ ($[A]_{j, j'} > 0$ for all $j, j'$) is not guaranteed to be positive semidefinite. Schoenberg's characterization \citep{schoenberg1938metric} provides a sharp condition: an exponential radial kernel $k(c, c') = \exp(-\alpha d^2(c, c'))$ is positive semidefinite for all bandwidths $\alpha > 0$ if and only if the underlying exponent $d^2$ is conditionally negative definite (CND). Because $-[\Theta]_{c, c'}$ fails the CND condition, naive exponentiation fails whenever off-diagonal correlations turn negative. In contrast, grounding categorical kernels on the squared Hilbertian chordal distance $d_\Theta^2(c, c') = 2(1 - [\Theta]_{c, c'})$ satisfies Schoenberg's condition, preserving entries in $(0, 1]$ while guaranteeing positive semidefiniteness.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Resolution via the Hilbertian Correlation Distance.}
To construct a distance-based categorical kernel satisfying Schoenberg's theorem, we utilize the canonical \textit{Hilbertian correlation distance} (also known as the chordal metric on the elliptope) \citep{haasdonk2004learning,Roustant,oune2021latent}.

\begin{definition}[Hilbertian Metric and Squared Correlation Distance]
\label{def:hilbert_dist}
Let $\Theta \in \mathcal{C}_L$ be an admissible correlation matrix ($\Theta \succ 0$, $[\Theta]_{j, j} = 1$). Since $\Theta \succ 0$, there exists a Cholesky factorization $\Theta = V V^\top$, where the column vectors $v_j \in \mathbb{R}^L$ satisfy $\|v_j\|_2 = \sqrt{[\Theta]_{j, j}} = 1$. 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The \textit{Hilbertian correlation metric} (chordal distance) between levels $c, c' \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ is:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:hilbert_metric}
    d_\Theta(c, c') := \|v_c - v_{c'}\|_2 = \sqrt{2\left(1 - [\Theta]_{c, c'}\right)}.
    \end{equation}
    \item The corresponding \textit{squared Hilbertian distance} is:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:hilbert_dist}
    d_\Theta^2(c, c') := \|v_c - v_{c'}\|_2^2 = \|v_c\|_2^2 + \|v_{c'}\|_2^2 - 2 \langle v_c, v_{c'} \rangle = 2\left(1 - [\Theta]_{c, c'}\right).
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Universal Schoenberg--Bernstein Class of Hilbertian Distance Categorical Kernels]
\label{th:hilbert_kernel_psd}
Let $\Theta \in \mathcal{C}_L$ be an admissible correlation matrix---obtained either under the Gershgorin sufficient condition $\rho_{\max} < \frac{1}{L-1}$ (Theorem~\ref{th:spd_condition}), or following post-hoc regularization via the repair operator $\Theta = \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}$ (Theorem~\ref{th:reg_spd}). Let $d_\Theta(c, c') = \sqrt{2(1 - [\Theta]_{c, c'})}$ denote the Hilbertian chordal distance on $\{1, \dots, L\}$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Hilbertian Embedding and CND Property:} The squared chordal distance $d_\Theta^2(c, c') = 2(1 - [\Theta]_{c, c'})$ is an exact squared Euclidean metric between linearly independent vectors on the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^L$, and is Conditionally Negative Definite (CND) on $\{1, \dots, L\}$.
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Universal Completely Monotonic Family:} Let $\varphi : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be any strictly completely monotonic function satisfying $\varphi(0) = 1$ (i.e., $(-1)^k \varphi^{(k)}(r) > 0$ for all $k \ge 0$ and $r > 0$). Then the radial categorical kernel:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:universal_dist_kernel}
    k_\varphi(c, c') := \varphi\bigl(d_\Theta^2(c, c')\bigr)
    \end{equation}
    generates a strictly positive definite Gram matrix ($K_\varphi \succ 0$) on any collection of distinct categorical observations.
    \item[(c)] \textbf{Canonical Covariance Classes:} In particular, strict positive definiteness holds for any categorical lengthscale $l_q > 0$ across all standard stationary covariance families:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item[(i)] \textbf{Gaussian / Squared Exponential:} $\varphi(r) = \exp\left(-\frac{r}{2 l_q^2}\right) = \exp\left(-\frac{1 - [\Theta]_{c, c'}}{l_q^2}\right)$;
        \item[(ii)] \textbf{Mat\'ern Family ($\nu > 0$):} With modified Bessel function $K_\nu$ and gamma function $\Gamma(\nu)$,
        \begin{equation}
        k_{\nu}(c, c') = \frac{2^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)} \left( \frac{\sqrt{2\nu} d_\Theta(c, c')}{l_q} \right)^\nu K_\nu\left( \frac{\sqrt{2\nu} d_\Theta(c, c')}{l_q} \right),
        \end{equation}
        yielding the closed-form Mat\'ern-3/2 and Mat\'ern-5/2 kernels:
        \begin{align}
        k_{3/2}(c, c') &= \left( 1 + \frac{\sqrt{3} d_\Theta(c, c')}{l_q} \right) \exp\left( - \frac{\sqrt{3} d_\Theta(c, c')}{l_q} \right), \\
        k_{5/2}(c, c') &= \left( 1 + \frac{\sqrt{5} d_\Theta(c, c')}{l_q} + \frac{5 d_\Theta^2(c, c')}{3 l_q^2} \right) \exp\left( - \frac{\sqrt{5} d_\Theta(c, c')}{l_q} \right);
        \end{align}
        \item[(iii)] \textbf{Rational Quadratic ($\alpha > 0$):} $k_{\mathrm{RQ}}(c, c') = \left( 1 + \frac{d_\Theta^2(c, c')}{2\alpha l_q^2} \right)^{-\alpha}$;
        \item[(iv)] \textbf{Power Exponential / Stable ($\gamma \in (0, 1]$):} $k_{\mathrm{PE}}(c, c') = \exp\left( - \left(\frac{d_\Theta^2(c, c')}{2 l_q^2}\right)^\gamma \right)$.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item[(d)] \textbf{Unit Diagonals and Correlation Range:} All kernels satisfy $k(c, c) = 1$ and off-diagonal entries $k(c, c') \in [\varphi(4), 1] \subset (0, 1]$ for all $c \neq c'$, strictly satisfying the Cauchy--Schwarz reproducing kernel bound.
    \item[(e)] \textbf{Coordinate-Wise PLS Expansion:} When $\Theta = \mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}) \in \mathcal{C}_L$ is admissible, $d_\Theta^2(c, c')$ expands coordinate-wise in PLS coordinates via the selection operator $\delta$ as:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:d_pls_expansion}
    d_{\mathcal{T}(\hat{\Theta})}^2(c, c') = \sum_{m=1}^{D_{\inp}} [\delta(c, c')]_m \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} \left([G_*]_{m, p}\right)^2 \hat{d}^2\bigl(\psi^{-1}(p, \ell)\bigr),
    \end{equation}
    where $\hat{d}^2(t, t') = 2(1 - [\hat{\Theta}]_{t, t'})$ denotes the latent squared Hilbertian distance.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For (a), the Cholesky factorization $\Theta = V V^\top$ constructs an exact Euclidean embedding $\{v_1, \ldots, v_L\} \subset \mathbb{R}^L$ with $d_\Theta^2(c, c') = \|v_c - v_{c'}\|_2^2$, which is Conditionally Negative Definite by Schoenberg's classical metric theorem \citep{schoenberg1938metric}. For (b), by Bernstein's theorem, completely monotonic functions are mixtures of Gaussian kernels; positive definiteness ($K_\varphi \succ 0$) on distinct levels follows from linear independence of Cholesky vectors and Micchelli's radial interpolation theorem \citep{micchelli1986interpolation}. Part (c) instantiates standard covariance classes, bounds in (d) follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:entry_bound}, and coordinate expansion (e) follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical_pls_recov}. The complete proof is detailed in Appendix~\ref{subsec:proof_hilbert}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Critical Requirement of Admissibility for Distance-Based Kernels]
\label{rem:admissibility_requirement}
The algebraic identity in \eqnref{eq:d_pls_expansion} holds coordinate-wise for any symmetric matrix $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta}) \in \mathcal{S}_1^L$. However, if $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})$ is indefinite, the embedding factorization $\Theta = V V^\top$ does not exist in real Euclidean space, and the quantity $d_{\mathcal{T}(\hat{\Theta})}^2$ is not conditionally negative definite. In such cases, evaluating $k_{\mathrm{dist}}$ directly on an un-repaired matrix can produce non-positive semidefinite Gram matrices. Therefore, in distance-based categorical kriging, applying the repair operator $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}$ of Theorem~\ref{th:reg_spd} (either exact or Gershgorin) is a prerequisite before evaluating the Hilbertian distance.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Architectural Validation: Row Norms and Definiteness Breakdown}

\paragraph{Empirical Behavior of Row Norms: Raw PLS vs. RN-PLS.}
In Section~\ref{sec:operators}, Remark~\ref{rem:enforce} established that raw, unconstrained PLS regression does not yield a rotation matrix $G_*$ with unit row norms. To quantify this in practice, we evaluated $N = 1{,}000$ randomized experimental designs across varying categorical levels $L \in \{5, 10, 15, 20\}$ and latent dimensions $\ell \in \{2, 3, 5\}$ (detailed in Table~\ref{tab:row_norms}, Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_row_norms}). If applied directly to latent correlations, reconstructed off-diagonal entries are multiplied by up to a factor of $24$, inevitably driving correlations outside $[-1, 1]$. In Gaussian process emulation benchmarks, un-normalized raw PLS experienced complete Cholesky decomposition failure in $100\%$ of trials with $L \ge 10$. Row normalization (Assumption~\ref{ass:unit_row_norm}) is therefore a mandatory structural prerequisite (RN-PLS) to enforce valid convex combinations on the correlation elliptope.

\subsection{Quasi-Newton Profile Likelihood Optimization and Computational Runtime}
\label{subsec:mle_optimization}

A central practical consideration in Gaussian process emulation is the numerical behavior of the regularization strategy within iterative Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE). In mixed continuous--categorical computer experiments, training observations $\bm{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_t}$ are modeled under a zero-mean Gaussian process prior $Y(x, c) \sim \mathcal{GP}(0, \sigma^2 k)$ with process variance $\sigma^2 > 0$ and a dimensionless relative regularizing nugget $\tau^2 \ge 0$ (such as $\tau^2 = 10^{-4}$ in benchmark tests, or numerical jitter $\delta_{\mathrm{jitter}} \approx 10^{-8}$ in deterministic interpolation). The complete training covariance factors as:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma(\bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta}) = \sigma^2 \left( R_{\mathrm{quant}}(\bm{\theta}) \odot \bigl(M \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}(\hat{\Theta}) M^\top\bigr) + \tau^2 I_{n_t} \right) = \sigma^2 K_{\mathrm{train}}(\bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta}).
\end{equation}
Because the relative nugget $\tau^2$ is a fixed dimensionless hyperparameter, the normalized training correlation matrix $K_{\mathrm{train}}(\bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta})$ is independent of the process variance $\sigma^2$. Differentiating the Gaussian log-likelihood:
\begin{equation}
\ln p(\bm{y} \mid \bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta}, \sigma^2) = -\frac{n_t}{2}\ln(2\pi) - \frac{n_t}{2}\ln(\sigma^2) - \frac{1}{2}\ln\det\bigl(K_{\mathrm{train}}(\bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta})\bigr) - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\bm{y}^\top K_{\mathrm{train}}(\bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta})^{-1}\bm{y}
\end{equation}
with respect to $\sigma^2$ yields the exact closed-form profile estimate $\hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{n_t} \bm{y}^\top K_{\mathrm{train}}(\bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta})^{-1} \bm{y}$. Substituting $\hat{\sigma}^2$ back concentrates out the process variance analytically, leaving the continuous lengthscales $\bm{\theta}$ and latent correlation parameters $\hat{\Theta}$ to be estimated by minimizing the concentrated negative profile log-likelihood:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:neg_log_lik}
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta}) = \frac{1}{2} \ln \det\bigl(K_{\mathrm{train}}(\bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta})\bigr) + \frac{n_t}{2} \ln\left( \bm{y}^\top K_{\mathrm{train}}(\bm{\theta}, \hat{\Theta})^{-1} \bm{y} \right) + \frac{n_t}{2}\bigl(1 + \ln(2\pi / n_t)\bigr),
\end{equation}
using quasi-Newton algorithms such as L-BFGS-B \citep{byrd1995limited}.

\paragraph{Regularization Strategies under Comparison.}
We investigated the impact of five distinct regularization strategies on numerical conditioning and predictive emulation accuracy when reconstructing non-SPD categorical matrices:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Raw Diagonal Nugget (SMT Heuristic):} Shifting the diagonal by an un-normalized nugget $\Theta_{\mathrm{nugget}} = \Theta + \eta I_L$. In the Gaussian process literature, two distinct variants of this heuristic exist:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Fixed Heuristic Jitter ($\eta_{\mathrm{fixed}} = 10^{-4}$):} Computationally trivial ($O(1)$), but \textbf{fails to guarantee positive definiteness} whenever $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) < -10^{-4}$. Because unregularized PLS reconstructions frequently exhibit eigenvalues as negative as $-17.87$ (Table~\ref{tab:non_spd_freq}), fixed jitter suffers Cholesky factorization failures in over $80\%$ of high-dimensional trials.
        \item \textit{Adaptive Minimal Shift ($\eta_{\mathrm{adapt}} = \max(0, -\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \varepsilon)$):} Guarantees strict positive definiteness with margin $\varepsilon$, but requires computing $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta)$, incurring an $O(L^3)$ eigenvalue decomposition (or $O(k L^2)$ Lanczos iterations)---hence it is \textit{not} computationally $O(1)$. Furthermore, both variants violate the unit-diagonal geometry of the correlation elliptope: $[\Theta_{\mathrm{nugget}}]_{j, j} = 1 + \eta \neq 1$, artificially inflating the process variance to $\sigma^2(1+\eta)$ and misaligning the signal-to-noise ratio against the observation noise $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$. In our benchmark comparisons, ``Raw Nugget'' denotes this adaptive minimal shift $\Theta + \eta_{\mathrm{adapt}} I_L$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spectral Clipping (SVD Projection):} Decomposing $\Theta = V \Lambda V^\top$, clipping $\lambda_i \leftarrow \max(\lambda_i, \varepsilon)$, and re-normalizing by $D^{-1/2} (V \Lambda_+ V^\top) D^{-1/2}$ where $D = \diag(V \Lambda_+ V^\top)$. This preserves unit diagonals but incurs an expensive $O(L^3)$ eigenvalue decomposition at each MLE likelihood evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Exact Spectral Repair (Theorem~\ref{th:reg_spd}):} $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \frac{\Theta + \eta_{\mathrm{exact}} I_L}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{exact}}}$ with $\eta_{\mathrm{exact}} = \max(0, -\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \varepsilon)$. This guarantees positive definiteness and unit diagonals, requiring $O(L^3)$ flops (or $O(k L^2)$ via Lanczos) to determine $\lambda_{\min}(\Theta)$.
    \item \textbf{Fast Gershgorin Repair (Theorem~\ref{th:reg_spd}):} $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \frac{\Theta + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} I_L}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}$ with $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} = \max\left(0, \max_j \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| - 1 + \varepsilon\right)$. This computes row sums in $O(L^2)$ flops without eigensolvers, guaranteeing unit diagonals and positive definiteness.
    \item \textbf{Mean-Deflated Gershgorin Repair (MDG, Theorem~\ref{th:reg_spd}):} $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \frac{\Theta + \eta_{\mathrm{MDG}^*} I_L}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{MDG}^*}}$ with point-wise envelope $\eta_{\mathrm{MDG}^*} = \min(\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}, \eta_{\mathrm{MDG}})$. By deflating the non-negative mean correlation $\bar{\rho}_+$, MDG eliminates the conservative over-shrinkage cap of naive Gershgorin repair on positive clusters while executing in $O(L^2)$ flops without eigensolvers.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Empirical Optimization Profiling and Computational Runtime.}
To evaluate whether the piecewise-smooth structure affects numerical convergence in practice, we recorded optimization diagnostics across 750 L-BFGS-B trajectories per method (50 replications $\times$ 5 multi-starts across $f_1, f_2, f_3$, totaling 3,750 runs). Fast Gershgorin repair and MDG achieved a \textbf{100.0\% convergence rate} across all 750 trajectories without encountering line-search failures or Cholesky breakdowns. In contrast, SVD spectral clipping achieved a 94.4\% convergence rate, while the raw diagonal nugget converged in only 86.0\% of runs. In finite-precision floating-point arithmetic, the un-normalized diagonal $[\Theta_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{j, j} = 1 + \eta$ distorts the block structure of $K_{\mathrm{train}}$, generating condition numbers an order of magnitude higher than certified repair ($\kappa \approx 10^6$ vs $7 \times 10^4$). During quasi-Newton line searches near the boundary of the admissible domain, these severe condition number spikes and rounding errors trigger numerical indefiniteness in standard Cholesky solvers, causing 14.0\% of trajectories to terminate prematurely. By preserving exact unit diagonals, Gershgorin and MDG repair strictly avoid this numerical failure mode. Moreover, L-BFGS-B required an average of $44.2 \pm 8.6$ iterations ($66.1 \pm 12.3$ likelihood evaluations) under Gershgorin repair, compared to $48.7 \pm 11.2$ iterations ($78.4 \pm 18.5$ evaluations) under Exact Spectral Repair, indicating that moderate shrinkage stabilizes line searches near the cone boundary.

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:repair_tradeoff}(b) (and further illustrated for large dimensions $L \le 80$ in Figure~\ref{fig:runtime_scaling}(a)), because Gershgorin and MDG repair rely solely on row operations ($O(L^2)$ flops) rather than iterative symmetric QR eigensolvers ($O(L^3)$ flops), the average wall-clock time per 5-start MLE fit dropped from $1.60 \pm 0.28$~s for Exact Spectral Repair and $2.23 \pm 0.35$~s for SVD Clipping down to \textbf{$0.42 \pm 0.07$~s} for Gershgorin repair and \textbf{$0.44 \pm 0.08$~s} for MDG. This represents a \textbf{73.8\% runtime reduction} relative to Exact Spectral Repair and an \textbf{81.2\% reduction} relative to SVD Clipping, confirming that the quadratic algebraic scaling translates directly into substantial computational speedups during Gaussian process training.

\paragraph{Analysis of Numerical Conditioning and Invariance.}
An important mathematical subtlety must be highlighted regarding numerical conditioning: for any positive scalar $c > 0$ and symmetric positive definite matrix $A$, the 2-norm spectral condition number is strictly invariant under scaling, $\kappa_2(c A) = \kappa_2(A)$. Consequently, for an isolated matrix with a fixed shift $\eta$,
\begin{equation}
\kappa_2(\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}) = \kappa_2\left(\frac{\Theta + \eta I_L}{1 + \eta}\right) = \kappa_2(\Theta + \eta I_L) = \frac{\lambda_{\max}(\Theta) + \eta}{\lambda_{\min}(\Theta) + \eta}.
\end{equation}
Therefore, the superior conditioning of Gershgorin repair does \textit{not} stem from the scalar divisor $1 + \eta$ itself, but from two interrelated mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Magnitude of Regularizing Shrinkage:} Because $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} \ge \eta_{\mathrm{exact}}$, the Gershgorin shift imposes a stronger diagonal shrinkage toward the identity $I_L$. Since the function $f(\eta) = \frac{\lambda_{\max} + \eta}{\lambda_{\min} + \eta}$ is strictly decreasing with $\lim_{\eta \to \infty} f(\eta) = 1$, larger shifts monotonically drive $\kappa_2$ closer to 1, effectively dampening extreme collinearity.
    \item \textit{Preservation of Signal-to-Noise Scale in $K_{\mathrm{train}}$:} In Gaussian process modeling, the complete training covariance matrix is $\Sigma = \sigma^2 K_{\mathrm{train}}$, where $K_{\mathrm{train}} = R_{\mathrm{quant}} \odot (M \Theta M^\top) + \tau^2 I_{n_t}$ and $\tau^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 / \sigma^2$ is the dimensionless noise-to-signal variance ratio (relative nugget). Because $\Sigma = \sigma^2 K_{\mathrm{train}}$, the condition number reported in Table~\ref{tab:reg_comparison} and Table~\ref{tab:multifunc_benchmark} satisfies $\kappa_2(\Sigma) = \kappa_2(K_{\mathrm{train}}).$ Under the raw nugget heuristic, $[\Theta_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{j, j} = 1 + \eta \neq 1$. This introduces two distinct pathologies: (a) for training pairs sharing the same categorical level $c_r = c_s$, $[K_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{r, s} = R_{\mathrm{quant}}(x_r, x_s)(1+\eta)$, whereas for distinct levels $c_r \neq c_s$, $[K_{\mathrm{raw}}]_{r, s} = R_{\mathrm{quant}}(x_r, x_s)[\Theta]_{c_r, c_s}$, artificially inflating within-category correlations relative to cross-category correlations by $(1+\eta)$; and (b) the effective diagonal entry is $1 + \eta + \tau^2$, dilating the signal variance to $\sigma^2(1+\eta)$ and misaligning the true relative noise ratio to $\frac{\tau^2}{1+\eta} \neq \tau^2$. In contrast, the exact unit diagonal $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{j, j} = 1$ strictly preserves both the stationary product kernel geometry and the true physical noise-to-signal ratio $\tau^2$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Analysis of Numerical Conditioning.}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:multifunc_benchmark} and visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:benchmark_boxplots}, the Gershgorin repair reduces the mean condition number $\kappa(K_{\mathrm{train}})$ by an order of magnitude across all three test functions---from $(8.0 - 9.5) \times 10^5$ under the raw diagonal nugget (and $(5.7 - 6.7) \times 10^5$ under SVD clipping) down to $(6.8 - 7.0) \times 10^4$. Notably, whereas the raw nugget suffers from condition number elevations ($\kappa(K_{\mathrm{train}}) > 1.5 \times 10^6$) in 10\% of the replications on $f_1$---inflating the mean test RMSE to $2.28 \pm 4.61$---the diagonal-preserving shrinkage of \eqnref{eq:regularization} eliminates these numerical outliers and yields the lowest median and mean RMSE across $f_1, f_2$, and $f_3$.

\paragraph{Predictive Volatility and Numerical Outliers under the Raw Nugget.}
Inspection of the error distributions across all 50 replications clarifies why the raw diagonal nugget strategy exhibits high predictive standard deviations ($2.28 \pm 4.61$ on $f_1$, $1.03 \pm 2.19$ on $f_2$, and $0.82 \pm 1.45$ on $f_3$). On $f_1$, exactly 5 out of 50 replications (10.0\%) under the raw nugget suffered near-singular condition number spikes ($\kappa(K_{\mathrm{train}}) \approx 1.5 \times 10^6 - 2.5 \times 10^6$), where floating-point error amplification during the linear solve produced test RMSEs exceeding 10.0 (up to 28.4). In terms of robust non-parametric statistics, the median test RMSE of the raw nugget on $f_1$ is 0.98 (IQR $[0.72, 1.45]$). In contrast, Gershgorin repair experienced zero near-singular breakdowns across all runs, yielding a maximum test RMSE of 1.18 and a median of 0.83 (IQR $[0.76, 0.91]$). Furthermore, the un-normalized shift $[\Theta_{\mathrm{nugget}}]_{j, j} = 1 + \eta$ violates the unit-diagonal geometry of the correlation elliptope, artificially inflating diagonal entries, dilating the effective signal variance to $\sigma^2(1+\eta)$, and misaligning the trade-off between categorical cross-correlations and continuous distance scales during MLE optimization.

\paragraph{Baseline Predictive Error and Additive Stress Testing on $f_1$.}
Across all five regularization methods in Table~\ref{tab:multifunc_benchmark}, the baseline test RMSE on $f_1$ ($0.83 - 0.98$) is noticeably higher than on $f_2$ ($0.18 - 0.38$) and $f_3$ ($0.12 - 0.22$). This occurs because the harmonic benchmark function $f_1(x, c) = \sin(2\pi x) + \cos(2\pi c/L) + 0.1 x (c/L)$ is fundamentally dominated by an additive superposition of a full-period continuous oscillation and a discrete categorical oscillation, whereas the Gaussian process emulator in \eqnref{eq:gp_model} employs a purely multiplicative (separable tensor product) covariance kernel $R_{\mathrm{quant}}(x, x') \, [\Theta]_{c, c'}$ with only $n_j = 3$ observations per level. Under a multiplicative kernel without an explicit additive decomposition, $n_j = 3$ training points per level cannot simultaneously resolve high-frequency continuous oscillations in $x$ while decoupling the independent additive categorical shifts. Thus, $f_1$ serves intentionally as an additive misspecification stress test for multiplicative covariance models, reinforcing the motivation for the additive and ANOVA kernel extensions outlined in Section~\ref{sec:conclu}.

\paragraph{Complementary Roles of Fast Gershgorin and Mean-Deflated Repair.}
The multi-function benchmark results in Table~\ref{tab:multifunc_benchmark} clarify the complementary roles of the two $O(L^2)$ repair operators. Fast Gershgorin repair applies conservative shrinkage $\alpha = \frac{\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}$ toward the identity $I_L$. In surrogate modeling, this provides an effective regularizing trade-off: dampening extreme off-diagonal correlations prevents near-collinear categorical representations and achieves the lowest condition number across all benchmarks ($\kappa \approx 6.8\times 10^4 - 7.0\times 10^4$), yielding the lowest RMSE when off-diagonal noise or ill-conditioning dominates ($0.19 \pm 0.06$ on $f_2$ and $0.13 \pm 0.06$ on $f_3$). Conversely, when genuine positive correlations cluster across categories, deflating the mean non-negative correlation $\bar{\rho}_+$ via Mean-Deflated Gershgorin (MDG) eliminates this conservative over-shrinkage cap. As demonstrated in Table~\ref{tab:multifunc_benchmark}, MDG closely matches the condition number of Exact Spectral Repair ($(1.22 - 1.25) \times 10^5$) while matching or improving upon Exact Spectral Repair in predictive emulation accuracy (median test RMSE $0.84$ on $f_1$, $0.21$ on $f_2$, and $0.14$ on $f_3$, compared to $0.92, 0.29$, and $0.21$ for Exact Repair). Notably, MDG achieves this parity in $O(L^2)$ flops without eigenvalue factorizations.

\subsection{Engineering Case Study: Mixed-Variable Structural Surrogate of an Aerospace Cantilever Beam}
\label{subsec:cantilever_benchmark}

To establish the practical engineering utility of PLS categorical dimension reduction and evaluate the spectral repair strategies on a realistic physical application, we investigated a mixed continuous--categorical structural mechanics benchmark: the tip deflection of an aerospace cantilever beam subjected to a vertical aerodynamic tip load $P_y = 15\,\mathrm{kN}$.

\paragraph{Physical Formulation and Mixed Design Space.}
The beam geometry is parameterized by $d = 3$ continuous design variables:
\begin{equation}
x = (L_{\mathrm{beam}}, b, h) \in [1.0, 2.5]\,\mathrm{m} \times [0.05, 0.20]\,\mathrm{m} \times [0.10, 0.35]\,\mathrm{m},
\end{equation}
governing the beam length $L_{\mathrm{beam}}$, base width $b$, and section height $h$. The material is selected from $L = 12$ qualitative structural material grades partitioned into four physical metallurgical and composite families:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Aluminum Alloys (Levels 1--3):} 2024-T3 ($E = 73.1\,\mathrm{GPa}$), 6061-T6 ($E = 68.9\,\mathrm{GPa}$), and 7075-T6 ($E = 71.7\,\mathrm{GPa}$);
    \item \textbf{Titanium Alloys (Levels 4--6):} Ti-6Al-4V ($E = 113.8\,\mathrm{GPa}$), Ti-5553 ($E = 110.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$), and Ti-6242 ($E = 117.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$);
    \item \textbf{Structural Steels (Levels 7--9):} AISI 4130 ($E = 205.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$), AISI 4340 ($E = 210.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$), and 17-4PH Stainless ($E = 196.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$);
    \item \textbf{High-Performance Alloys and Composites (Levels 10--12):} Inconel 718 ($E = 205.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$), CFRP Quasi-Isotropic ($E = 60.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$), and CFRP High-Modulus ($E = 135.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$).
\end{enumerate}
The physical quantity of interest is the maximum vertical tip deflection $\delta_{\mathrm{tip}}(x, c)$, derived from Euler--Bernoulli beam theory:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cantilever_deflection}
\delta_{\mathrm{tip}}(x, c) = \frac{P_y L_{\mathrm{beam}}^3}{3 E(c) I_z(b, h)} = \frac{4 P_y L_{\mathrm{beam}}^3}{E(c) b h^3},
\end{equation}
where $E(c)$ is the elastic modulus determined by the discrete material index $c \in \{1, \dots, 12\}$. Across the design domain, tip deflections vary non-linearly from $0.033\,\mathrm{mm}$ up to $312.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ (domain mean $24.8\,\mathrm{mm}$, standard deviation $32.1\,\mathrm{mm}$). Relative to this wide physical variation, the achieved test RMSEs of $6.44 - 7.19\,\mathrm{mm}$ represent high predictive accuracy ($R^2 \approx 0.95$, normalized RMSE $\mathrm{NRMSE} \approx 0.14 - 0.22$).

In each replication, $n_t = 36$ training points ($3$ observations per material grade) were sampled using Latin Hypercube Sampling on the continuous inputs with a balanced categorical design, corrupted by additive Gaussian observation noise $\sigma_{\varepsilon} = 0.05\,\mathrm{mm}$ ($1\%$ relative noise). \paragraph{Engineering Interpretation: Why MDG Excels on Material Clusters.}
The cantilever benchmark results in Table~\ref{tab:cantilever_benchmark} provide a compelling empirical demonstration of Theorem~\ref{th:reg_spd}(d). Because the 12 material grades are organized into metallurgical families with nearly identical elastic moduli (e.g., all aluminum alloys have $E \approx 70\,\mathrm{GPa}$ and all structural steels have $E \approx 200\,\mathrm{GPa}$), the true qualitative correlation matrix $\Theta$ features strong positive intra-cluster correlation blocks ($\rho \approx 0.85 - 0.95$). Under naive Fast Gershgorin repair, the row sum $\sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}|$ is heavily dominated by these positive clusters, forcing an aggressive Gershgorin shift $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$ and applying extreme shrinkage $\alpha \to 1$ toward the identity matrix. While this drives the condition number down to $1.79 \times 10^2$, it severely damps the genuine intra-family correlations, treating distinct aluminum or steel grades as independent materials and inflating the median test RMSE to $9.06\,\mathrm{mm}$.

In contrast, Mean-Deflated Gershgorin (MDG) deflates the mean positive correlation $\bar{\rho}_+$, recognizing that uniform positive correlation blocks do not threaten positive definiteness. By shifting only against the mean-deflated residual $\Delta$, MDG eliminates the conservative over-shrinkage cap of naive Gershgorin repair. Consequently, MDG achieves the lowest median test RMSE of all methods ($\mathbf{6.44\,\mathrm{mm}}$), outperforming SVD clipping ($6.89\,\mathrm{mm}$) and Exact Spectral Repair ($6.68\,\mathrm{mm}$), while executing in \textbf{$0.29\,\mathrm{s}$} (a $4\times$ computational speedup over Exact Spectral Repair and SVD clipping). This confirms that MDG provides the ideal combination of $O(L^2)$ evaluation efficiency and accurate physical cluster preservation for real-world engineering surrogate modeling. As visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:runtime_scaling}(b), the learned correlation matrix $[\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{c, c'}$ reconstructs the four distinct metallurgical blocks with high fidelity, demonstrating that RN-PLS with MDG autonomously recovers the physical domain structure.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclu}

In this paper, we have established the geometric and spectral foundations of Partial Least Squares dimension reduction for categorical correlation functions in Gaussian process emulation. Motivated by the challenge of scaling qualitative kriging to high-dimensional categorical problems, our analysis resolves the positive definiteness question left open by \citet{saves2023high}. We demonstrated that high-dimensional categorical dimension reduction is governed by the geometry of the correlation elliptope $\mathcal{C}_L$. Under the Row-Normalized PLS (RN-PLS) architecture, the reconstruction operator acts as a convex combination on cross-level correlation entries, preserving the hypercube bounds $[-1, 1]$. However, preserving entrywise bounds does not guarantee positive definiteness: latent compression preserves the elliptope over an order-of-magnitude broader parameter domain than reconstruction, and the emergence of indefinite reconstructions reflects the dimensional contraction of the negative correlation boundary $-1/(L-1) \to 0^-$ as dimension grows. On this negative boundary, the Gershgorin condition of Theorem~\ref{th:spd_condition} is sharp. Furthermore, the round-trip operator $\mathcal{P} = \mathcal{R}_W \circ \mathcal{T}_{G_*}$ acts as a row-stochastic Markov transition operator with equicorrelation fixed points and Dobrushin dispersion non-expansion $\tau(M) \le 1$, ensuring bounded iterative transformations without dispersion explosion, while the admissible latent domain $\mathcal{A}_L$ is an open, bounded, convex set whose closure is a spectrahedron.

These theoretical results provide direct algorithmic principles for surrogate modeling libraries, such as the Surrogate Modeling Toolbox \citep{saves2023smt}. First, a clear distinction must be maintained between adding an observational noise nugget $\sigma_\varepsilon^2 I_{n_t}$ to the training Gram matrix and applying an un-normalized diagonal shift directly to the qualitative correlation matrix $\Theta_{\mathrm{nugget}} = \Theta + \eta I_L$. The latter violates the unit-diagonal geometry of the correlation elliptope, artificially inflating the marginal process variance to $\sigma^2(1+\eta)$ and precipitating severe condition number breakdowns during maximum likelihood estimation. Instead, the certified diagonal-preserving repair $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \frac{\Theta + \eta I_L}{1 + \eta}$ should be adopted, executing in $O(L^2)$ flops without eigensolvers and lowering covariance condition numbers by an order of magnitude. Moreover, rather than relying on numerical finite differences during quasi-Newton profile likelihood optimization, software implementations can deploy the closed-form directional Jacobian of the repair operator (Theorem~\ref{th:gershgorin_gradient}). Finally, for distance-based categorical kernels, naive entrywise exponentiation violates conditionally negative definiteness whenever correlations turn negative; valid distance kernels must instead be grounded on the squared Hilbertian chordal metric $d_\Theta^2(c, c') = 2(1 - [\Theta]_{c, c'})$ on certified positive definite reconstructions.

Several natural avenues emerge for future research. While this work prioritized separable multiplicative kernels ($R_{\mathrm{quant}} \times R_{\mathrm{qual}}$), general mixed covariance structures can be constructed via additive or functional ANOVA decompositions \citep{Roustant}. Extending the PLS projection and Gershgorin bounds to additive models will allow simultaneous estimation of distinct variance proportions and multi-way functional interactions. Building on the closed-form heteroscedastic covariance repair established in Corollary~\ref{cor:heteroscedastic_repair} ($\Sigma_{\mathrm{SPD}} = (\Sigma + \eta D^2)/(1+\eta)$), a second perspective involves jointly estimating the category-dependent scales $D = \diag(\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_L)$ and the latent PLS correlations within the concentrated profile likelihood. Finally, when categorical inputs possess natural physical or chemical hierarchies, such as the periodic table groupings in the nuclear waste benchmark of \citet{Roustant}, hybridizing structured block-constant priors with supervised PLS reduction offers an attractive blend of physical domain knowledge and data-driven dimension reduction.

\subsection{Proof of Graph Incidence Factorization and Rank Bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_bound})}
\label{subsec:proof_rank_bound}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_bound}]
\textit{Proof of (a).}
The $i$-th row of the cross-level design matrix $X$ corresponds to observation $c_i \in \{1, \dots, L\}$. By Definition~\ref{def:cross_level}, $[X]_{i, \cdot} = \zeta_{c_i}^\top = e_{c_i}^\top E$, where $e_{c_i} \in \{0, 1\}^L$ is the standard basis indicator of category $c_i$. Stacking row-wise gives $X = M E \in \mathbb{R}^{n_t \times D_{\inp}}$.

For the incidence Gram identity, consider the $(j, j')$-th entry of $E E^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L}$:
\begin{equation}
[E E^\top]_{j, j'} = \sum_{r=1}^{D_{\inp}} [E]_{j, r} [E]_{j', r}.
\end{equation}
Because $E$ is the unoriented vertex-edge incidence matrix of the complete graph $K_L$, $[E]_{j, r} = 1$ if and only if edge $r$ is incident to vertex $j$.
When $j = j'$, vertex $j$ has degree $L - 1$ in $K_L$, which yields:
\begin{equation}
[E E^\top]_{j, j} = \operatorname{deg}(j) = L - 1.
\end{equation}
When $j \neq j'$, vertices $j$ and $j'$ share exactly one incident edge in $K_L$ (namely the unordered pair $\{j, j'\}$), so:
\begin{equation}
[E E^\top]_{j, j'} = 1 \quad (j \neq j').
\end{equation}
Expressing this in matrix notation yields:
\begin{equation}
E E^\top = (L - 1) I_L + (\mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top - I_L) = (L - 2) I_L + \mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top.
\end{equation}
To determine its spectral decomposition, observe that $\mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top \mathds{1}_L = L \mathds{1}_L$. Therefore, $\mathds{1}_L$ is an eigenvector:
\begin{equation}
E E^\top \mathds{1}_L = (L - 2) \mathds{1}_L + L \mathds{1}_L = 2(L - 1) \mathds{1}_L,
\end{equation}
giving the dominant eigenvalue $\lambda_1(E E^\top) = 2(L - 1) > 0$ with multiplicity $1$.
For any vector $u \in \mathds{1}_L^\perp$ orthogonal to $\mathds{1}_L$ ($u^\top \mathds{1}_L = 0$), the rank-one term vanishes:
\begin{equation}
E E^\top u = (L - 2) u.
\end{equation}
Thus, the orthogonal complement $\mathds{1}_L^\perp$ is an eigenspace of dimension $L - 1$ associated with the repeated eigenvalue $\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_L = L - 2$.
For $L \ge 3$, $L - 2 > 0$, so all $L$ eigenvalues of $E E^\top$ are strictly positive. Hence $E E^\top \succ 0$, establishing $\operatorname{rank}(E) = L$ directly. Moreover, for any $u \in \mathds{1}_L^\perp$,
\begin{equation}
\|E^\top u\|_2^2 = u^\top E E^\top u = (L - 2) \|u\|_2^2 \implies \|E^\top u\|_2 = \sqrt{L - 2} \|u\|_2,
\end{equation}
so $E^\top$ acts as a scaled homothety (isometry up to $\sqrt{L - 2}$) on the centered subspace $\mathds{1}_L^\perp$.

\textit{Proof of (b).}
Centering $X = M E$ by the centering projection matrix $H_{n_t} = I_{n_t} - \frac{1}{n_t} \mathds{1}_{n_t} \mathds{1}_{n_t}^\top$ gives $\tilde{X} = H_{n_t} M E$. The sample covariance Gram matrix is:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{X} \tilde{X}^\top = H_{n_t} M E E^\top M^\top H_{n_t} = H_{n_t} M \bigl((L - 2) I_L + \mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top\bigr) M^\top H_{n_t}.
\end{equation}
Because every row of the assignment matrix $M$ contains exactly one 1, $M \mathds{1}_L = \mathds{1}_{n_t}$.
Since $H_{n_t} \mathds{1}_{n_t} = \bm{0}$, the second term vanishes identically:
\begin{equation}
H_{n_t} M (\mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top) M^\top H_{n_t} = (H_{n_t} \mathds{1}_{n_t}) (H_{n_t} \mathds{1}_{n_t})^\top = \bm{0}_{n_t \times n_t}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, we obtain the exact factorization:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:appendix_centered_gram}
\tilde{X} \tilde{X}^\top = (L - 2) H_{n_t} M M^\top H_{n_t}.
\end{equation}
Because $L \ge 3$, $L - 2 > 0$, so $\operatorname{rank}(\tilde{X} \tilde{X}^\top) = \operatorname{rank}(H_{n_t} M M^\top H_{n_t}) = \operatorname{rank}(H_{n_t} M)$.
The uncentered assignment matrix $M \in \{0, 1\}^{n_t \times L}$ has rank equal to the number of observed levels $L_{\mathrm{obs}}$.
Since $\mathds{1}_{n_t} = M \mathds{1}_L \in \operatorname{col}(M)$, the rank-nullity theorem implies that the centering operator $H_{n_t}$, whose kernel is $\operatorname{span}(\mathds{1}_{n_t})$, deflates the rank of $M$ by exactly 1:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{rank}(\tilde{X}) = \operatorname{rank}(H_{n_t} M) = L_{\mathrm{obs}} - 1 \le \min(n_t - 1, L - 1).
\end{equation}

\textit{Proof of (c).}
In a balanced design with pure categorical inputs, each of the $L$ levels appears exactly $n_t / L$ times, so $M^\top M = \frac{n_t}{L} I_L$.
The non-zero eigenvalues of $\tilde{X} \tilde{X}^\top = (L - 2) H_{n_t} M M^\top H_{n_t}$ coincide with those of $(L - 2) M^\top H_{n_t} M$.
Using $M^\top \mathds{1}_{n_t} = \frac{n_t}{L} \mathds{1}_L$, we have:
\begin{equation}
M^\top H_{n_t} M = M^\top M - \frac{1}{n_t} (M^\top \mathds{1}_{n_t})(M^\top \mathds{1}_{n_t})^\top = \frac{n_t}{L} I_L - \frac{n_t}{L^2} \mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top = \frac{n_t}{L} \left( I_L - \frac{1}{L} \mathds{1}_L \mathds{1}_L^\top \right) = \frac{n_t}{L} H_L,
\end{equation}
where $H_L$ is the orthogonal centering projector in $\mathbb{R}^L$. Thus:
\begin{equation}
(L - 2) M^\top H_{n_t} M = \frac{n_t(L - 2)}{L} H_L.
\end{equation}
Because $H_L$ is an orthogonal projector of rank $L - 1$, its eigenvalues are 0 (multiplicity 1, eigenvector $\mathds{1}_L$) and 1 (multiplicity $L - 1$).
Therefore, the Gram matrix $\tilde{X} \tilde{X}^\top$ possesses a single non-zero eigenvalue $\lambda = \frac{n_t(L - 2)}{L}$ of multiplicity $L - 1$.
In univariate PLS with response $\tilde{\bm{y}} = H_{n_t} \bm{y}$, the $m$-th Krylov subspace is defined as $\mathcal{K}_m(\tilde{X} \tilde{X}^\top, \tilde{\bm{y}}) = \operatorname{span}\{\tilde{\bm{y}}, (\tilde{X}\tilde{X}^\top)\tilde{\bm{y}}, \dots, (\tilde{X}\tilde{X}^\top)^{m-1}\tilde{\bm{y}}\}$.
Because $(\tilde{X} \tilde{X}^\top)^k \tilde{\bm{y}} = \lambda^k \tilde{\bm{y}}$ for any response vector lying in $\operatorname{col}(\tilde{X})$, the Krylov subspace is strictly 1-dimensional:
\begin{equation}
\dim \mathcal{K}_m(\tilde{X}\tilde{X}^\top, \tilde{\bm{y}}) = 1 \quad \forall m \ge 1.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the univariate NIPALS algorithm deflates $\tilde{X}$ to zero after extracting the very first component ($t_1 \propto \tilde{\bm{y}}$), rendering any subsequent PLS component extraction impossible without coupling categorical variables to continuous inputs or introducing hierarchical structure.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Spectral Similarity and Nullspace Structure of Sample Gram Matrices (Proposition~\ref{prop:gram_psd})}
\label{subsec:proof_gram}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:gram_psd}]
\textit{Proof of (a).} For any arbitrary vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^{n_t}$, set $u = M^\top v \in \mathbb{R}^L$. Then the quadratic form evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
v^\top K v = v^\top (M \Theta M^\top) v = u^\top \Theta u \ge 0,
\end{equation}
since $\Theta \succ 0$ is Symmetric Positive Definite. Thus $K \succeq 0$. Furthermore, because $\Theta \succ 0$, $u^\top \Theta u = 0$ if and only if $u = M^\top v = \bm{0}_L$. Hence $\ker(K) = \ker(M^\top)$, which by the rank-nullity theorem yields:
\begin{equation}
\rank(K) = \rank(M \Theta M^\top) = \rank(M^\top) = \rank(M) = \min(n_t, L_{\mathrm{obs}}),
\end{equation}
where $L_{\mathrm{obs}} \le L$ is the number of distinct categorical levels present in the sample. Therefore, $K \succ 0$ is strictly positive definite if and only if $n_t \le L$ and all observations exhibit distinct categorical levels ($L_{\mathrm{obs}} = n_t$).

\textit{Proof of (b).} By Sylvester's cyclic determinant theorem, for any matrices $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n_t \times L}$ and $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times n_t}$, the non-zero eigenvalues of $X Y$ coincide with the non-zero eigenvalues of $Y X$. Setting $X = M$ and $Y = \Theta M^\top$, we have $X Y = M \Theta M^\top = K$ and $Y X = \Theta (M^\top M) = \Theta D_n$. Transposing, the eigenvalues of $\Theta D_n$ are identical to those of $B = D_n \Theta$.
Let $D_n^{1/2} = \diag(\sqrt{n_1}, \ldots, \sqrt{n_L}) \ge 0$. The matrix $S = D_n^{1/2} \Theta D_n^{1/2}$ is real symmetric and positive semidefinite ($S \succeq 0$). On the subspace of observed categories $\{j : n_j > 0\}$, $D_n^{1/2}$ is invertible, establishing the similarity transformation:
\begin{equation}
D_n^{1/2} B D_n^{-1/2} = D_n^{1/2} (D_n \Theta) D_n^{-1/2} = D_n^{1/2} D_n^{1/2} \Theta D_n^{1/2} D_n^{-1/2} = S.
\end{equation}
Because $B$ is similar to the real symmetric matrix $S$, all $L_{\mathrm{obs}}$ non-zero eigenvalues of $B$ are strictly real and strictly positive.

\textit{Proof of (c).} When the experimental design is balanced, $n_j = n_t / L$ for all $j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$, so $D_n = \frac{n_t}{L} I_L$. Thus $B = \frac{n_t}{L} \Theta$, which immediately implies that the $L$ non-zero eigenvalues of $K$ are scaled copies $\lambda_i(K) = \frac{n_t}{L} \lambda_i(\Theta)$ for $i \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$, while the remaining $n_t - L$ eigenvalues vanish.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Universal Strict Positive Definiteness of Hilbertian Distance Kernels (Theorem~\ref{th:hilbert_kernel_psd})}
\label{subsec:proof_hilbert}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:hilbert_kernel_psd}]
\textit{Proof of (a).} Since $\Theta \in \mathcal{C}_L$ is Symmetric Positive Definite, there exists a unique lower-triangular Cholesky factor $V \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L}$ with strictly positive diagonal entries such that $\Theta = V V^\top$. Letting $v_j \in \mathbb{R}^L$ denote the $j$-th row vector of $V$, we have $\langle v_j, v_{j'} \rangle = [V V^\top]_{j, j'} = [\Theta]_{j, j'}$. In particular, $\|v_j\|_2^2 = [\Theta]_{j, j} = 1$. The squared Hilbertian chordal distance evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
d_\Theta^2(c, c') = \|v_c - v_{c'}\|_2^2 = \|v_c\|_2^2 + \|v_{c'}\|_2^2 - 2 \langle v_c, v_{c'} \rangle = 2(1 - [\Theta]_{c, c'}).
\end{equation}
Because $d_\Theta^2(c, c') = \|v_c - v_{c'}\|_2^2$ is an exact squared Euclidean metric between points on the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^L$, classical metric embedding theory \citep[Theorem~1]{schoenberg1938metric} establishes that $d_\Theta^2$ is Conditionally Negative Definite (CND) on $\{1, \dots, L\}$.

\textit{Proof of (b).} By Bernstein's theorem on completely monotonic functions, a function $\varphi : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ is completely monotonic if and only if it is the Laplace transform of a non-negative Borel measure $\mu$ on $[0, \infty)$:
\begin{equation}
\varphi(r) = \int_0^\infty e^{-r s} \, \mathrm{d}\mu(s).
\end{equation}
For each fixed $s > 0$, the kernel $(c, c') \mapsto \exp\bigl(-s d_\Theta^2(c, c')\bigr) = \exp\bigl(-s \|v_c - v_{c'}\|_2^2\bigr)$ is positive semidefinite by Schoenberg's theorem \citep{schoenberg1938metric}.
Integrating over $s$ preserves positive semidefiniteness: for any vector $u \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and distinct levels $c_1, \dots, c_n$,
\begin{equation}
u^\top K_\varphi u = \sum_{r, r'=1}^n u_r u_{r'} \int_0^\infty e^{-s d_\Theta^2(c_r, c_{r'})} \, \mathrm{d}\mu(s) = \int_0^\infty \left( \sum_{r, r'=1}^n u_r u_{r'} e^{-s \|v_{c_r} - v_{c_{r'}}\|_2^2} \right) \mathrm{d}\mu(s) \ge 0.
\end{equation}
To establish strict positive definiteness ($K_\varphi \succ 0$), note that $\det(\Theta) = (\det(V))^2 > 0$ implies that the Cholesky vectors $\{v_1, \dots, v_L\}$ are linearly independent and hence mutually distinct ($v_c \neq v_{c'}$ for $c \neq c'$).
By Micchelli's radial basis function interpolation theorem \citep{micchelli1986interpolation}, for any strictly completely monotonic function (where $\mu$ is not concentrated at $0$), the matrix $[e^{-s \|v_{c_r} - v_{c_{r'}}\|_2^2}]_{r, r'=1}^n$ is strictly positive definite for all $s > 0$.
Since $\mu((0, \infty)) > 0$, integrating strictly positive quadratic forms over $(0, \infty)$ yields $u^\top K_\varphi u > 0$ for all $u \neq \bm{0}$, establishing $K_\varphi \succ 0$.

\textit{Proof of (c).} The Gaussian, Mat\'ern, Rational Quadratic, and Power Exponential kernels are classical examples of strictly completely monotonic functions of the squared Euclidean distance \citep{williams2006gaussian,schoenberg1938metric,micchelli1986interpolation}:
for the Mat\'ern family, $\varphi_\nu(r) = \frac{2^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)} (\sqrt{2\nu r})^\nu K_\nu(\sqrt{2\nu r})$ is strictly completely monotonic on $[0, \infty)$ for all $\nu > 0$;
for the Rational Quadratic family, $\varphi(r) = (1 + r / (2\alpha))^{-\alpha} = \frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \int_0^\infty s^{\alpha - 1} e^{-s} e^{-s r / (2\alpha)} \, \mathrm{d}s$ is a continuous mixture of Gaussian kernels, hence strictly completely monotonic;
for the Power Exponential family with $\gamma \in (0, 1]$, $r \mapsto r^\gamma$ is a Bernstein function with completely monotonic derivative, ensuring that $\exp(-r^\gamma)$ is strictly completely monotonic.

\textit{Proof of (d) \& (e).}
For (d), unit diagonals follow from $d_\Theta^2(c, c) = 0 \implies \varphi(0) = 1$. Since $[\Theta]_{c, c'} \in [-1, 1]$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:entry_bound}, $d_\Theta^2(c, c') \in [0, 4]$. Monotonicity of $\varphi$ ensures $k_\varphi(c, c') \in [\varphi(4), 1] \subset (0, 1]$.
For (e), substituting the linear reconstruction formula $[\Theta]_{j, j'} = \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*]_{\psi(j, j', L), p})^2 [\hat{\Theta}]_{\psi^{-1}(p, \ell)}$ into $d_\Theta^2(j, j') = 2(1 - [\Theta]_{j, j'})$ and using $\sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*]_{r, p})^2 = 1$ yields:
\begin{align}
d_\Theta^2(j, j') &= 2 \left( \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*]_{r, p})^2 - \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*]_{r, p})^2 [\hat{\Theta}]_{\psi^{-1}(p, \ell)} \right) \nonumber \\
&= \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*]_{r, p})^2 \cdot 2\bigl(1 - [\hat{\Theta}]_{\psi^{-1}(p, \ell)}\bigr) = \sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} ([G_*]_{r, p})^2 \hat{d}^2\bigl(\psi^{-1}(p, \ell)\bigr),
\end{align}
which establishes \eqnref{eq:d_pls_expansion}.
\end{proof}

\section{Non-Smooth Analysis of the Fast Gershgorin Shift Operator}
\label{sec:appendix_nonsmooth}

In Section~\ref{subsec:repair_operators} and Section~\ref{subsec:mle_optimization}, the fast Gershgorin repair operator $\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} = \frac{\Theta + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} I_L}{1 + \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}}$ relies on the shift function:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app_eta_gersh}
\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) = \max\left(0,\; \max_{1 \le j \le L} \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\mathcal{T}_{G_*}(\hat{\Theta})]_{j, j'}| - 1 + \varepsilon\right).
\end{equation}
In this appendix, we formalize the subdifferential properties of $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$ and provide the theoretical foundation for its stable behavior in quasi-Newton optimization.

\subsection{Clarke Generalized Subdifferential and Polyhedral Chamber Geometry}

\begin{proposition}[Clarke Subdifferential and Almost-Everywhere Differentiability]
\label{prop:clarke_subdiff}
Let $\hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$ denote the free latent correlation vector.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Global Lipschitz Continuity:} The shift function $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}} : \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is globally Lipschitz continuous with Euclidean Lipschitz constant:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lipschitz_bound}
    \mathrm{Lip}_2(\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}) \le L - 1.
    \end{equation}
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Polyhedral Partition:} The parameter space $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$ is partitioned into a finite collection of closed polyhedral chambers $\{\mathcal{P}_k\}_{k=1}^K$ on the interior of which $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$ is an exact affine function:
    \begin{equation}
    \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) = a_k^\top \hat{\bm{\theta}} + b_k, \quad \forall \hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \operatorname{int}(\mathcal{P}_k).
    \end{equation}
    \item[(c)] \textbf{Measure-Zero Transition Boundary:} Non-differentiability occurs exclusively on the boundaries $\partial \mathcal{P}_k$, which are contained in a finite union of hyperplanes and have Lebesgue measure zero:
    \begin{equation}
    \mu\left( \bigcup_{k=1}^K \partial \mathcal{P}_k \right) = 0.
    \end{equation}
    \item[(d)] \textbf{Clarke Subdifferential Characterization:} At any point $\hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$, the Clarke generalized gradient $\partial_C \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}(\hat{\bm{\theta}})$ is the convex hull of the active affine gradients:
    \begin{equation}
    \partial_C \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) = \operatorname{conv}\left\{ a_k : \hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathcal{P}_k \right\}.
    \end{equation}
    In particular, for almost every $\hat{\bm{\theta}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$, $\partial_C \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) = \{ \nabla \eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) \}$ is a singleton matching the closed-form gradient in Theorem~\ref{th:gershgorin_gradient}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For (a), the reconstruction operator $\mathcal{T}_{G_*}$ assigns weights $w_{j, j'}^p = ([G_*]_{\psi(j, j', L), p})^2 \ge 0$ satisfying $\sum_{p=1}^{D_{\outp}} w_{j, j'}^p = 1$. The gradient of the linear coordinate mapping $\hat{\bm{\theta}} \mapsto [\Theta]_{j, j'}$ is the weight vector $w_{\psi(j, j', L)}$, which satisfies $\|w_{\psi(j, j', L)}\|_2 \le \|w_{\psi(j, j', L)}\|_1 = 1$. On any smooth chamber, the row sum $g_j(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) = \sum_{j' \neq j} |[\Theta]_{j, j'}| - 1 + \varepsilon$ has gradient $\nabla g_j(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) = \sum_{j' \neq j} \operatorname{sgn}([\Theta]_{j, j'}) w_{\psi(j, j', L)}$. By the triangle inequality, $\|\nabla g_j(\hat{\bm{\theta}})\|_2 \le \sum_{j' \neq j} \|w_{\psi(j, j', L)}\|_2 \le L - 1$. Since the pointwise maximum of a finite family of Lipschitz functions preserves the Lipschitz constant, Rademacher's theorem guarantees that $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$ is globally $(L-1)$-Lipschitz with respect to the Euclidean norm, and differentiable almost everywhere.

For (b) \& (c), the polyhedral chambers are formed by the intersections of the affine half-spaces corresponding to the sign choices of off-diagonal entries $\{[\Theta]_{j, j'} \gtrless 0\}$, the pairwise comparisons of Gershgorin row radii $\{g_j(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) \gtrless g_{j'}(\hat{\bm{\theta}})\}$, and the non-negativity threshold $\{g_j(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) \gtrless 0\}$. Because the coordinate mapping $\hat{\bm{\theta}} \mapsto [\Theta]_{j, j'}$ is affine, each of these conditions defines an affine hyperplane in $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$. Consequently, the parameter space $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$ is partitioned into a finite polyhedral complex $\{\mathcal{P}_k\}_{k=1}^K$. On the interior of each chamber $\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{P}_k)$, the active row index $j^*$ and all coordinate signs $\operatorname{sgn}([\Theta]_{j^*, j'})$ remain strictly constant, rendering $\eta_{\mathrm{Gersh}}$ an exact affine function. The transition boundaries $\partial \mathcal{P}_k$ are contained in a finite union of affine hyperplanes of dimension $D_{\outp} - 1$, and therefore have Lebesgue measure zero in $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$.

For (d), Clarke's theorem for max-type functions of smooth constituents \citep[Theorem~2.3.9]{clarke1990optimization} establishes that the generalized gradient is the convex hull of the gradients of the active functions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact Derivation and Full Gradient of the Profile Log-Likelihood}
\label{subsec:full_mle_gradient}
In Gaussian process regression with mixed quantitative and qualitative inputs, the training observations $\bm{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_t}$ follow:
\begin{equation}
\bm{y} \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\bm{0}, \Sigma\right), \quad \text{with} \quad \Sigma = \sigma^2 R_{\mathrm{train}} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 I_{n_t},
\end{equation}
where $[R_{\mathrm{train}}]_{i, j} = R_{\mathrm{quant}}(x_i, x_j) [\Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}]_{c_i, c_j}$ represents the certified correlation structure. To profile out the process variance $\sigma^2$ analytically, the observation noise is parameterized via the dimensionless noise-to-signal variance ratio (relative nugget) $\tau^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 / \sigma^2 \ge 0$, so that the covariance matrix factors as:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma = \sigma^2 \left( R_{\mathrm{train}} + \tau^2 I_{n_t} \right) = \sigma^2 K_{\mathrm{train}}(\bm{\theta}_{\mathrm{quant}}, \hat{\bm{\theta}}).
\end{equation}
Under this parameterization, the negative log-likelihood of the observations is:
\begin{equation}
-2 \ln p(\bm{y} \mid \bm{\theta}_{\mathrm{quant}}, \hat{\bm{\theta}}, \sigma^2) = n_t \ln(2\pi) + n_t \ln(\sigma^2) + \ln \det(K_{\mathrm{train}}) + \frac{1}{\sigma^2} \bm{y}^\top K_{\mathrm{train}}^{-1} \bm{y}.
\end{equation}
Setting $\frac{\partial}{\partial \sigma^2} (-2\ln p) = 0$ yields the exact concentrated maximum likelihood estimator:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma_hat_concentrated}
\hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{n_t} \bm{y}^\top K_{\mathrm{train}}^{-1} \bm{y}.
\end{equation}
Substituting $\hat{\sigma}^2$ back into the negative log-likelihood eliminates $\sigma^2$ analytically, yielding the profile negative log-likelihood $\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{\mathrm{quant}}, \hat{\bm{\theta}})$ given in \eqnref{eq:neg_log_lik}.

In Section~\ref{subsec:mle_optimization}, quasi-Newton algorithms (such as L-BFGS-B) minimize $\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{\mathrm{quant}}, \hat{\bm{\theta}})$. Given the exact analytical Jacobian $\nabla_{\hat{\bm{\theta}}} \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L \times D_{\outp}}$ derived in Theorem~\ref{th:gershgorin_gradient}, the complete gradient of $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to latent correlation parameter $\theta_p$ ($p \in \{1, \ldots, D_{\outp}\}$) is obtained in closed form via the matrix chain rule:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:full_likelihood_gradient}
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta_p} = \frac{1}{2} \Tr\left( \left( K_{\mathrm{train}}^{-1} - \frac{n_t}{\bm{y}^\top K_{\mathrm{train}}^{-1} \bm{y}} K_{\mathrm{train}}^{-1} \bm{y} \bm{y}^\top K_{\mathrm{train}}^{-1} \right) \frac{\partial K_{\mathrm{train}}}{\partial \theta_p} \right),
\end{equation}
where the derivative of $K_{\mathrm{train}} = R_{\mathrm{quant}} \odot (M \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}} M^\top) + \tau^2 I_{n_t}$ is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cov_deriv_chain}
\frac{\partial K_{\mathrm{train}}}{\partial \theta_p} = R_{\mathrm{quant}} \odot \left( M \frac{\partial \Theta_{\mathrm{SPD}}}{\partial \theta_p} M^\top \right).
\end{equation}
Once the Cholesky factorization of $K_{\mathrm{train}}$ is computed in $O(n_t^3)$ flops, evaluating the full gradient vector $\nabla_{\hat{\bm{\theta}}} \mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\outp}}$ requires only $O(D_{\outp} n_t^2)$ flops. This completely eliminates finite-difference gradient approximations, providing exact numerical directions for quasi-Newton solvers.
